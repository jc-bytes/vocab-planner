# 8th Grade Technology - Simple Classroom Progress

This file explains the year in simple classroom sentences. It is meant for quick teacher review, parent-friendly explanation, or student-facing overview.

## 1st Trimester

### March

- Week 1, Class 1: First, students work on Introduction to vector graphics. They open Inkscape, create simple shapes, and practice fills and strokes without adding a separate vocabulary table.
- Week 1, Class 2: Then, students work on Shapes, layers, object order, and the Trimester 1 summative vocabulary. They complete Daily Grade #1: Create a vocabulary table with definition, illustration, and example sentence for these 10 words: vector graphic, bitmap, fill, stroke, z-order, shape, layer, object, path, node. They also create a simple environmental badge.
- Week 2, Class 1: Then, students work on Alignment, distribution, and grouping. They use alignment and distribution tools to organize repeated objects in a pattern or border.
- Week 2, Class 2: Then, students work on Paths and nodes. They create straight and curved paths with the pen tool.
- Week 3, Class 1: Then, students work on Campaign graphic planning. They create a planning sketch for one campaign icon or logo.
- Week 3, Class 2: Then, students work on Campaign icon or logo draft. They complete Daily Grade #2: Create one original campaign icon or logo in Inkscape with at least 8 objects, clear fill and stroke choices, at least 2 grouped parts, at least 1 edited path, and a one-sentence campaign message.
- Week 4, Class 1: Then, students work on SVG and markup basics. They edit one simple SVG property such as color, size, or position in a guided example.
- Week 4, Class 2: Then, students work on Design review and improvement. They complete Appreciation Grade #1: Complete a collaboration checklist about responsibility, communication, respect, organization, and use of class time during peer review. Improve their campaign graphic using at least 2 peer or self-review notes.

### April

- Week 5, Class 1: Then, students work on Computing systems. They practice labeling a computing systems diagram and draft one sentence explaining how the parts work together before the graded diagram check.
- Week 5, Class 2: Then, students work on Operating systems and logical thinking. They complete Daily Grade #3: Label a computing systems diagram with processor, memory, storage, input, output, communication, operating system, and program; add one sentence explaining how the parts work together to run a website. They also create simple logic examples that connect to search and filtering.
- Week 6, Class 1: Then, students work on AI and machine learning. They test Google Teachable Machine or an approved AI demo.
- Week 6, Class 2: Then, students work on Open source and responsible remixing. They explore how viewing code helps people learn.
- Week 7, Class 1: Then, students work on HTML structure. They write a simple campaign page with a heading, two paragraphs, one list, and one hyperlink.
- Week 7, Class 2: Then, students work on Images and CSS styling. They complete Daily Grade #4: Create one campaign web page using HTML and CSS with a title, two sections, one image with alt text, one list, one link, three CSS style rules, and a short source credit.
- Week 8, Class 1: Then, students work on Search engines and keywords. They create 5 improved search queries using AND, OR, NOT, quote search, or the minus sign.
- Week 8, Class 2: Then, students work on Portfolio content and video organization. They complete Appreciation Grade #2: Complete a self-reflection about effort, organization, digital responsibility, receiving feedback, and improving work during the web and research unit. Create a Google Sites portfolio map with Home, Campaign, Vector Graphics, Research/Credits, and Reflection pages. Create a 5-shot storyboard or outline for the required environmental video with topic, message, visuals, narration/text, and source needs.

### May

- Week 9, Class 1: Then, students work on Project evidence check. They practice planning the portfolio map and identify the pages, graphics, video location or storyboard status, source credits, and call to action needed.
- Week 9, Class 2: Then, students work on Google Sites setup. They complete Daily Grade #5: Create a portfolio planning map with at least 5 pages, required content for each page, 3 original vector graphics to include, required environmental video location or storyboard status, 2 source credits, and one campaign call to action. They also create the site shell with Home, Campaign, Vector Graphics, Research/Credits, and Reflection pages.
- Week 10, Class 1: Then, students work on Exam project launch. They begin Exam Project: Create or revise the homepage with a clear campaign title, short purpose statement, call to action, and one original vector graphic.
- Week 10, Class 2: Then, students work on Portfolio build and media integration. They continue Exam Project: Build the Campaign and Vector Graphics pages. Add at least 3 original vector graphics, the required environmental video, captions, and short explanations of design choices.
- Week 11, Class 1: Then, students work on Testing and feedback. They continue Exam Project: Fix at least 3 issues found during testing or peer feedback. Add missing alt text, captions, credits, or navigation links.
- Week 11, Class 2: Then, students work on Final revision and reflection. They continue Exam Project: Complete the Research/Credits and Reflection pages. Write a short reflection explaining what students made, what vector techniques students used, what challenge students solved, and how their site supports the campaign.
- Week 12, Class 1: Then, students work on Presentation setup. They rehearse the Google Sites environmental campaign portfolio presentation so the final presentation is ready for the long class.
- Week 12, Class 2: Then, students work on Presentation continuation. They complete Exam Project Presentation: Finish remaining presentations and submit the final site link, reflection, and any required files.
- Week 13, Class 1: Then, students work on Exam/admin buffer. They submit missing file links, revise incomplete reflection prompts, or complete assigned exam/admin forms.
- Week 13, Class 2: Then, students work on Trimester reflection and archive. They organize their digital portfolio folder, submit any missing exam/admin form, and write a reflection about the project process and skills learned.

## 2nd Trimester

### June

- Week 1, Class 1: Then, students work on Arduino and Freenove kit introduction. They inspect the kit, identify parts, and practice safe handling without adding a separate vocabulary table.
- Week 1, Class 2: Then, students work on LED blink and the Trimester 2 summative vocabulary. They complete Daily Grade #1: Create a vocabulary table with definition, illustration, and example sentence for these 10 words: Arduino, Freenove, microcontroller, circuit, breadboard, pin, power, ground, resistor, LED. They also build the LED Blink circuit.
- Week 2, Class 1: Then, students work on Flowing LED sequence. They build or simulate a flowing LED pattern with multiple LEDs.
- Week 2, Class 2: Then, students work on Digital input with a button. They build a button and LED circuit.
- Week 3, Class 1: Then, students work on Button logic and state changes. They practice building or tracing a button-controlled LED circuit and draft the input-process-output explanation before the graded build check.
- Week 3, Class 2: Then, students work on Serial monitor basics. They complete Daily Grade #2: Build and demonstrate a button-controlled LED circuit where the LED changes state when the button is pressed; submit a labeled photo or diagram and one sentence explaining the input, process, and output. They also upload a serial communication sketch.
- Week 4, Class 1: Then, students work on Debugging circuits. They debug a provided or recreated LED/button circuit using a checklist: power, ground, resistor, LED direction, pin number, code upload, and cable.
- Week 4, Class 2: Then, students work on Safe build challenge. They complete Appreciation Grade #1: Complete a safe material use and teamwork checklist about responsibility, communication, careful handling, organization, and perseverance during the build challenge. Build a small LED/button interaction and document it with a diagram.

### July

- Week 5, Class 1: Then, students work on Digital vs analog signals. They build or inspect an ADC/potentiometer setup.
- Week 5, Class 2: Then, students work on Potentiometer data and LED brightness. They complete Daily Grade #3: Create a data chart with at least 6 potentiometer positions, analog readings, LED brightness observations, and one conclusion sentence about the relationship between input and output.
- Week 6, Class 1: Then, students work on Photoresistor and environmental input. They test a photoresistor circuit and record at least 4 light readings.
- Week 6, Class 2: Then, students work on RGB LED and color output. They build or test an RGB LED circuit.
- Week 7, Class 1: Then, students work on Buzzer output. They create a simple buzzer alert pattern.
- Week 7, Class 2: Then, students work on Servo control. They complete Daily Grade #4: Build or demonstrate a servo or buzzer system controlled by a button, potentiometer, or sensor; submit a labeled diagram, the input used, the output behavior, and one explanation sentence.
- Week 8, Class 1: Then, students work on Conditional logic with sensors. They write or modify conditional code that changes an output based on a sensor threshold.
- Week 8, Class 2: Then, students work on Integrated practice challenge. They complete Appreciation Grade #2: Complete a self-reflection about perseverance, organization, careful testing, response to feedback, and improvement during Arduino work. Build and test one integrated practice challenge using at least one input and one output.

### August

- Week 9, Class 1: Then, students work on Ultrasonic sensing. They test an ultrasonic ranging sketch.
- Week 9, Class 2: Then, students work on Project design plan. They complete Daily Grade #5: Create an Arduino project design plan with problem/purpose, individual or pair role, input component, output component, circuit diagram, expected behavior, 3 test cases, and materials list.
- Week 10, Class 1: Then, students work on Exam project launch. They begin Exam Project: Build the first version of their circuit and upload a starter sketch. Label the input, process, and output in their notebook.
- Week 10, Class 2: Then, students work on Prototype building and code. They continue Exam Project: Program the prototype so the sensor or input controls at least one output. Test the circuit with 3 conditions and record the results. Debug wiring or code using a checklist.
- Week 11, Class 1: Then, students work on Testing and debugging. They continue Exam Project: Fix at least one wiring, code, or design issue. Record the problem, likely cause, and solution.
- Week 11, Class 2: Then, students work on Improvement and documentation. They continue Exam Project: Improve the prototype, complete a labeled circuit diagram, prepare a short explanation of the code logic, and collect final testing evidence.
- Week 12, Class 1: Then, students work on Demonstration setup. They rehearse the Arduino prototype demonstration so the final presentation is ready for the long class.
- Week 12, Class 2: Then, students work on Demonstration continuation. They complete Exam Project Presentation: Finish remaining demonstrations and submit their diagram, code evidence, testing notes, and reflection.
- Week 13, Class 1: Then, students work on Exam/admin buffer. They submit missing project uploads, revise incomplete reflection prompts, or complete assigned exam/admin forms.
- Week 13, Class 2: Then, students work on Trimester reflection and cleanup. They organize the kit, clean the workspace, and complete any required reflection or exam task.

## 3rd Trimester

### September

- Week 1, Class 1: Then, students work on Decomposition and app purpose. They break one app idea into smaller tasks without adding a separate vocabulary table.
- Week 1, Class 2: Then, students work on Interface planning and the Trimester 3 summative vocabulary. They complete Daily Grade #1: Create a vocabulary table with definition, illustration, and example sentence for these 10 words: decomposition, user, screen, event, input, output, app, button, variable, feedback. They also create a 3-screen wireframe.
- Week 2, Class 1: Then, students work on Events and screen flow. They build or trace a simple event map showing how the user moves from one app screen to another.
- Week 2, Class 2: Then, students work on Tappy Tap-style app practice. They modify a simple app so a button starts the activity, a timer or event changes the screen, and a score or message appears.
- Week 3, Class 1: Then, students work on User needs and feedback. They complete a user-needs chart with audience, problem, feature, and success criteria for their app idea.
- Week 3, Class 2: Then, students work on App plan and event map. They complete Daily Grade #2: Create an app screen plan and event map with at least 3 screens, 5 interface elements, 4 events, 1 variable or user input, and 3 test cases.

### October

- Week 4, Class 1: Then, students work on Algorithms and first Python output. They write and run simple Python print statements.
- Week 4, Class 2: Then, students work on Variables and input. They write short programs that ask for a name or number, store it in a variable, and print a customized response.
- Week 5, Class 1: Then, students work on Numbers and arithmetic. They practice writing a Python program that asks for numbers, calculates results, displays labeled output, and includes one comment before the graded programming task.
- Week 5, Class 2: Then, students work on Selection with if/else. They complete Daily Grade #3: Write a Python program that asks for two numbers, calculates at least 3 results, displays labeled output, and includes one comment explaining the program purpose. They also create a Python decision program using if/else.
- Week 6, Class 1: Then, students work on Random numbers and multi-branch choices. They modify a guided program that uses random numbers and if/elif/else.
- Week 6, Class 2: Then, students work on Loops and counters. They build a small loop program that repeats until a condition is met.
- Week 7, Class 1: Then, students work on Debugging selection and loops. They practice finding likely errors in a Python program with variables, if/else, and a loop before the graded debugging check.
- Week 7, Class 2: Then, students work on Scratch + Micro:bit setup. They complete Daily Grade #4: Debug a Python program with 5 marked or hidden errors involving variables, if/else, and a loop; submit the corrected code and a list of the 5 fixes. They also connect or simulate a Micro:bit with Scratch.
- Week 8, Class 1: Then, students work on Sensor-controlled game planning. They create a game concept plan with purpose, player goal, Micro:bit input, sprite response, scoring or success condition, and one possible challenge.
- Week 8, Class 2: Then, students work on Prototype and habits check. They complete Appreciation Grade #1: Complete a pair programming and debugging checklist about communication, participation, perseverance, respectful feedback, and organized testing. Build a first Scratch + Micro:bit prototype with one working sensor-controlled action.

### November

- Week 9, Class 1: Then, students work on Representation and binary digits. They practice vocabulary matches, small decimal/binary conversions, and examples of how devices represent information before the graded check.
- Week 9, Class 2: Then, students work on Project readiness and final planning. They complete Daily Grade #5: Complete a representation and binary check with 6 vocabulary matches, 6 decimal-to-binary or binary-to-decimal conversions using small numbers, and 2 short examples of how devices represent information. They also create the final game plan with title, individual or pair role, goal, controls, scoring or win condition, sprites, and 3 test cases.
- Week 10, Class 1: Then, students work on Exam project launch. They begin Exam Project: Create the Scratch game shell with title, main sprite, background, and one Micro:bit input mapped to a sprite action.
- Week 10, Class 2: Then, students work on Game building and sensor control. They complete Appreciation Grade #2: Complete a project readiness self-reflection about responsibility, organization, effort, safe device use, feedback, and improvement. They also continue Exam Project: Build the main game loop, sensor-controlled movement, scoring or success condition, and one challenge or obstacle.
- Week 11, Class 1: Then, students work on Debugging and user testing. They continue Exam Project: Ask a peer to test their game and record one control issue, one clarity issue, and one positive feature. Fix at least one issue.
- Week 11, Class 2: Then, students work on Documentation and presentation prep. They continue Exam Project: Finish the game, prepare screenshots or live demo evidence, write speaking notes for a 1:30-2 minute presentation, and complete the reflection prompts.
- Week 12, Class 1: Then, students work on Presentation setup. They rehearse the Scratch + Micro:bit game presentation so the final presentation is ready for the long class.
- Week 12, Class 2: Then, students work on Presentation continuation. They complete Exam Project Presentation: Continue presentations and submit the final Scratch project, reflection, and any required screenshots or notes.

### December

- Week 13, Class 1: Then, students work on Presentation make-up and exam/admin work. They present any missing project demo, submit missing project evidence, or complete assigned exam/admin forms.
- Week 13, Class 2: Then, students work on Trimester reflection and file cleanup. They complete a final reflection about their strongest skill, biggest challenge, debugging strategy, and one improvement for future projects.
