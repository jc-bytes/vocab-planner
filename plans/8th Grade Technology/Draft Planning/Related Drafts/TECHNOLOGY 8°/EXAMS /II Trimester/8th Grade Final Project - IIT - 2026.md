Robot Maze Challenge

8th Grade Final Project

Second Trimester

## Project Overview

In this project, you'll become a robotics engineer and problem-solver. Your mission is to rescue a robot that's trapped in a complex maze by programming it with the intelligence to find its way out autonomously.

## The Challenge Scenario

Imagine your mBot robot has wandered into a maze and needs your help to escape. The maze environment contains:

Walls that create barriers and block certain paths

Obstacles scattered throughout that must be navigated around

Ground lines that serve as helpful navigation guides

Dead ends that will test your robot's problem-solving logic

An exit that represents freedom and success

## Your Robot's Mission Objectives

### Primary Goal

Program your mBot to autonomously navigate from the maze entrance to the exit using sensor-based decision making and logical pathfinding.

### Core Behaviors Your Robot Must Master

1. Forward Movement & Line Detection

Begin by moving forward from the starting position

Continuously scan for ground lines using the light sensor

When a line is detected, engage line-following mode

2. Line Following Strategy

Track along detected lines smoothly and accurately

Maintain line contact until reaching an intersection or line termination

Use sensor feedback to make micro-adjustments for precise following

3. Obstacle Avoidance & Path Selection

Monitor the ultrasonic sensor for walls or obstacles ahead

When blocked, stop immediately and assess available options

Implement a consistent decision-making rule (such as "left-hand rule" or "right-hand rule")

4. Dead End Recovery

Recognize when all forward paths are blocked

Execute a 180-degree turn to reverse direction

Return to the last decision point to try alternative routes

5. Success Recognition

Identify the exit condition (exit line or open area)

Celebrate successful maze completion!

## Your Development Process

Phase 1: Logic Design (Flowchart) Create a detailed flowchart that maps out every decision your robot will make. Think through scenarios like:

What happens when the robot encounters its first line?

How does it choose between left and right at intersections?

What's the recovery strategy when it hits a dead end?

Phase 2: Robot Helper Concept - Real-World Applications Explain how your maze-solving robot could be helpful in real-life situations.

Choose one real-world application and explain how your maze-solving logic would make this robot truly helpful to people!

Consider these applications:

Emergency & Rescue Scenarios

Search and rescue robots navigating through collapsed buildings or disaster zones

Fire department robots exploring dangerous areas to locate trapped people

Military robots clearing safe paths through hazardous terrain

Healthcare & Assistance

Hospital robots delivering medicine and supplies through complex hospital corridors

Elderly care robots helping navigate homes and providing assistance

Robots assisting people with visual impairments by mapping safe walking routes

Industrial & Commercial Uses

Warehouse robots efficiently moving inventory through storage facilities

Cleaning robots navigating office buildings and public spaces

Security patrol robots monitoring large facilities and outdoor areas

Exploration & Research

Mars rovers exploring unknown planetary terrain

Underground mining robots mapping cave systems and tunnels

Ocean floor exploration robots navigating underwater obstacles

Phase 3: Programming (mBlock) Transform your flowchart logic into working code using mBlock's visual programming environment. Focus on:

Sensor input processing (ultrasonic, light sensors)

Movement control (motors, turning, speed)

Decision-making logic (if/then statements, loops)

Integration of all behaviors into one cohesive program

Phase 3.1: Testing & Iteration Test your robot in the actual maze environment:

Observe how it handles different maze sections

Identify areas where the logic needs refinement

Debug and improve your code based on real-world performance

Phase 4: Presentation (1.5-2 minutes) Prepare a compelling demonstration that showcases:

Your chosen real-world application and why it matters

Key features of your navigation algorithm (logic)

Key features of your code

How this technology could genuinely help people in real situations
