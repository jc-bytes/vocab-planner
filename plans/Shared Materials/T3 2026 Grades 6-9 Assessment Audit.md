## Standard now applied

- Each student direction names the exact product, counts, source or starter, practiced module sections, filename or paper label, submission evidence, checklist, and make-up path.
- Students receive fixed data, source cards, formulas, scenarios, option cards, or starter files. No formal task requires an open-web search.
- A rubric cannot introduce a requirement that is absent from the student directions and provided materials.
- A required deliverable maps to a scoring criterion or an explicit completion/submission check.
- Every 40-point assessment reserves exactly 4 points for punctuality, responsibility, and readiness. Every 90-point exam reserves exactly 9 points. A forgotten or uncharged personal device, unprepared login, lateness, or repeated responsibility issue may lower only this criterion. Academic criteria score the work itself.
- A documented school network, platform, teacher-provided account, or hardware failure outside the student's control uses equivalent evidence and does not reduce the readiness criterion when the assigned fallback is followed.
- No assessment requires a phone, photograph, camera, or screenshot.
- STEAM work from October 5–23 produces two individual Technology grades for every student. Grade 6 records Individual Work Process as Summative 3 and Expo Explanation and Closure as Summative 4. Grades 7–9 record Expo Explanation and Closure as Daily Grade 3 and Individual Work Process as Daily Grade 4. Daily Grade 5 remains the third regular Technology Daily grade. This numbering is intentionally nonchronological. STEAM is never an Appreciation grade in Grades 7–9. Students continue the one project assigned by Arts, Mathematics, Science, or Technology; no second STEAM product is created.
- Grades 7–9 also record two separate regular Technology Appreciation grades and one Exam. Those three grades use their own announced Technology evidence and are not replaced by STEAM participation.
- The first STEAM grade scores the student's individual preparation, contribution, testing, and work record. The second scores the assigned expo role, explanation, closure, and reflection. The project topic itself is not scored by Technology.

## Content boundaries

- Grade 6 charts use only the practiced column-chart workflow. SUM and AVERAGE remain module practice and are not silently added to the five formal summatives.
- Grade 7 spreadsheet work uses only the fixed dataset, `SUM`, `AVERAGE`, `MAX`, and the announced column chart. Mandrake work uses the taught `distance > 18 cm` rule.
- Grade 8 Python uses only practiced fundamentals; representation checks are limited to the binary concepts taught in that pathway. The exam uses the fixed Catch the Star specification and tests.
- Grade 9 image and sound work uses only the named module formulas and meanings. Cybersecurity uses fixed scenarios and records `R1–R6`. The proposal is an exam milestone, and the exam uses one of four fixed option cards.

## Verification summary

- The shared calendar and STEAM kit now use one category rule: Grade 6 STEAM Summatives 3 and 4; Grades 7–9 STEAM Daily grades 3 and 4; Daily Grade 5 is regular Technology work; Grades 7–9 Appreciation grades remain separate.
- Grade 6 has five active directions and five matching canonical DOCX/Classroom XLSX rubrics, including STEAM Summatives 3 and 4.
- Grade-level Grade 7–9 generators, rubrics, mirrors, and package audits must pass their own DOCX, Classroom, hash, render, and link checks after adopting this category correction. Do not reuse a previous validation result that classified STEAM as Appreciation.
