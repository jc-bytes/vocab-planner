# T3 2026 STEAM Work and Expo Kit

Use this kit during STEAM preparation from October 5–16 and STEAM Week from October 19–23 in Grades 6–9. Students continue the project assigned by Mathematics, Arts, Science, or Technology. They do not start a second Technology project or make a separate Technology copy of the group product.

Technology records two individual 40-point STEAM grades during these three weeks for every student, including students whose assigned project belongs to Mathematics, Arts, or Science. The project topic is not scored by Technology. Technology scores the student's own planning, contribution, testing, explanation, reflection, and responsible evidence connected to the one assigned project.

| Level | Complete T3 Technology grade structure | Category of the two STEAM grades |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 6 | Exactly 5 Summatives: 3 regular Technology Summatives and 2 STEAM Summatives | Individual Work Process is Summative 3. Expo Explanation and Closure is Summative 4. Neither is an Appreciation grade. |
| Grades 7–9 | Exactly 5 Daily grades: 3 regular Technology Daily grades and 2 STEAM Daily grades, plus 2 separate regular Technology Appreciation grades and 1 Exam | Expo Explanation and Closure is Daily Grade 3. Individual Work Process is Daily Grade 4. Daily Grade 5 remains the third regular Technology Daily grade. Neither STEAM grade is an Appreciation grade or the Exam. |

The individual work-process checkpoint takes place during October 12–16. The expo explanation and closure grade centers on the October 22 expo and must be completed by October 23. Grade-level packages publish the exact group checkpoint date and grade number.

The school STEAM Expo is Thursday, October 22, from 8:30–11:30 a.m.; organization and cleanup run from 11:30 a.m.–12:30 p.m. Groups have no more than eight students, and each class should have approximately three to four assigned projects. Projects should include a practical, creative, experimental, or demonstrative component. Expo explanations address what was created, the problem, how it works, what students learned, and how it can help make the world better.

Each grade reserves 4 of 40 points for readiness. A forgotten or uncharged personal computer, unprepared login, lateness, or repeated responsibility reminders may lower only those 4 points; the academic criteria score the Technology evidence itself.

A documented school network, platform, teacher-provided account, or hardware failure outside the student's control uses the printable fallback. This rule applies to every student, including students whose assigned STEAM project is with Mathematics, Arts, or Science.

The teacher monitors preparation, participation, role completion, safe and respectful work, file organization, dated individual work-log evidence, the assigned speaking or demonstration role, cleanup, and reflection.

## Evidence rules

- Students use their own computers.
- Personal mobile devices are not used.
- Accept saved files, share links, typed records, teacher observation, and live demonstrations.
- Each group keeps one assigned project, one current product, and one shared progress log. Individual work logs, observations, tests, explanations, and reflections provide the evidence for each student's Technology grade regardless of project subject.
- If the internet or a device fails, students complete the printable records and continue planning, explaining, testing, or documenting the assigned project.
