## Readiness standard

A class is ready when its row identifies:

1. the exact learning target;
2. the teacher guide or class note;
3. the projected module section or class visual;
4. the student task or work file;
5. the checkpoint and expected evidence;
6. the collection and checking method;
7. the make-up route;
8. the offline or device-failure fallback.

For a formal assessment, the post and attachments must also contain every source, dataset, starter, scenario, formula boundary, template, test input, filename, submission item, checklist condition, and scoring requirement. The rubric may explain performance levels but may not introduce a hidden requirement.

Modules remain classroom pilots until real completion, confusion, device, and accessibility evidence is collected. Their printable fallbacks should be available before class begins.
