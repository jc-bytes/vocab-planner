## Grade-Band Rule

4th grade is primary education.

Planning rule:
- Each trimester should have exactly 5 formal grades.
- No exam project or final project is required by default.
- Other activities should remain formative practice, guided exploration, review, feedback, or make-up work.
- Gradeable work should be short, concrete, visible, and supported by prior practice.

## Executive Finding

The first-trimester annual plan has a usable 4th-grade spine, but the March-May monthly plans are not yet reliable as official classroom implementation.

The strongest annual sequence is:
1. Internet and networks
2. Google Drive organization
3. Google Docs collaboration
4. Google Sheets data organization
5. Canva visual communication
6. Responsible AI awareness
7. Scratch-style repetition in shapes
8. Decomposition and simple program creation

The monthly plans only partly follow that sequence. March begins well with Internet/network topics, but then moves into copyright and fact-checking instead of Google Drive. April introduces Physical Architecture of Computers, which is not in the first-trimester annual spine. May focuses heavily on loops, decomposition, conditionals, variables, and constants, which makes the month too abstract and too dense for 4th grade if taught as written.

The level needs a correction pass before it should become the basis for rubrics, study guides, or generated summatives.

## Detailed Comparison

### March

What works:
- Connecting networks and internet components match annual Week 1.
- Sharing information and website structure match annual Week 2.
- Activities are mostly concrete: draw diagrams, label parts, list examples, share ideas.

Problems:
- March plan has 2025 date labels inside a 2026 plan.
- The annual plan expects Google Drive organization after Internet basics, but March uses copyright and fact-checking instead.
- Fact-checking asks students to find two sources and support an opinion. That is useful but high for 4th grade unless the teacher gives controlled websites or printed examples.
- March learning outcomes refer to audio recording/editing, but March lessons are about the Internet. This is a header/content mismatch.

Recommended adjustment:
- Keep Internet/network lessons.
- Keep copyright/fact-checking only as a short digital citizenship bridge.
- Add Google Drive organization before any major submission task.
- Use Summative #1 as a controlled vocabulary/Internet check after students practice the same words.

### April

What works:
- Physical computer parts can be age-appropriate if taught as label, match, and function.
- The review and assessment structure is concrete.
- Programming screen turtle and patterns connect to the annual programming strand.

Problems:
- Physical Architecture of Computers is not in the annual first-trimester spine.
- Hardware assessment becomes a formal grade without annual support unless the annual plan is updated.
- Dates are still 2025, and the Easter/no-class placement appears copied from the old calendar.
- Text-based turtle commands and programming letters may be too difficult if students have not first used visual block-based steps.
- April labels Summative Activity 4 before Summative #3 is clearly placed.

Recommended adjustment:
- Either remove hardware from T1 or update the annual plan to include a short "computer systems" bridge.
- If hardware stays, make it formative or a small worksheet, not one of the 5 formal grades unless the annual plan is updated.
- Replace text-based turtle syntax with Scratch-style blocks or teacher-projected pseudocode.
- Move loops/patterns after students have had simpler Drive/Docs/Canva tasks.

### May

What works:
- Loops and shape repetition are a good fit for 4th grade when visual and guided.
- Decomposition is appropriate when connected to real-life tasks and simple algorithms.
- Summative #5 can work if evidence is visible: step list, pseudocode, or a simple Scratch sequence.

Problems:
- "Using Loops to Create Shapes" repeats several times without clear progression or different evidence.
- Summative Activity 4 appears in April and again in May.
- Conditionals, IF/ELSE, variables, and constants are packed after decomposition. That is too much abstraction for one month unless heavily simplified.
- Robot examples appear in May, but robotics is the second-trimester annual focus. This creates a trimester leak.
- Resources list only pencil and notebook for lessons that require Scratch or a programming app.

Recommended adjustment:
- Keep loops and decomposition.
- Remove variables/constants from T1 or make them optional enrichment.
- Move conditionals into the robotics/programming trimester unless needed as a very simple teacher-led demo.
- Convert the end of May into review, make-up, and evidence collection for the 5 primary grades.

## Correct Difficulty Level for 4th Grade

The plan should follow this Grade 4 difficulty model:
- Start with concrete objects, pictures, and simple diagrams.
- Use short vocabulary sets, usually 4-6 words per lesson.
- Require drawings, labels, matching, sorting, short answers, guided templates, and simple digital products.
- Use block-based programming before text-based syntax.
- Teach one new abstraction at a time: loop, condition, variable, and procedure should not all be formal expectations in the same month.
- Keep student research controlled with approved websites, screenshots, or teacher-selected examples.
- Use presentations as short sharing or demonstrations, not major projects.

Content that is currently appropriate:
- Network diagrams with router/server/device labels
- Website parts and safe sharing
- Google Drive file organization
- Google Docs formatting and collaboration
- Google Sheets simple tables/sorting/counting
- Canva poster/map/design with a checklist
- AI examples and "AI can make mistakes" discussion
- Scratch loops for shapes
- Breaking down real-life tasks into steps

Content that needs simplifying:
- Configuring IP addresses
- Analyzing network traffic
- Creating basic web pages with HTML
- Open-ended fact-checking research
- Text-based turtle syntax
- Programming letters with angles and distances
- Procedures, IF/ELSE, variables, and constants as independent expectations

Content that should probably move out of T1:
- Full Physical Architecture of Computers assessment
- Robotics decision examples
- Variables and constants in programming
- IF/ELSE programming in an app, unless treated as a short guided preview

## Teacher Feasibility Check

Overall feasibility rating: not ready as written, but very fixable.

Main teacher risks:
1. Calendar mismatch. March and April use 2025 dates inside 2026 files.
2. Missing clean T1 coverage map. The annual has 13 weeks, but only 9 content weeks are clearly described.
3. Off-spine April hardware content. This creates extra teaching, extra assessment, and unclear annual alignment.
4. Tool/resource mismatch. Lessons mention Scratch, programming environments, Canva, Google tools, and hardware, but resource lists often say only notebook, pencil, headphones, or account/password.
5. Assessment numbering problem. Summative #1, #2, #4, and #5 are visible, but #3 is not clearly placed; #4 appears twice.
6. Difficulty clustering. May includes loops, decomposition, conditionals, variables, constants, pseudocode, algorithms, procedures, and robotics examples in quick succession.

Teacher reality judgment:
- March is teachable after date cleanup and a Google Drive insertion.
- April needs the biggest repair because its first half is an off-spine hardware unit and its second half jumps into programming.
- May should become the consolidation month: loops, decomposition, final checks, make-ups, and evidence completion.
