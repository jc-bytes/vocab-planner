## Executive Verdict

The 6th grade plan is feasible and close to teach-ready. The curriculum sequence is coherent, the formal assessment count follows the primary education rule of exactly five counted grades per trimester, and the monthly lessons generally keep one main purpose per class. The plan is strongest when it uses concrete evidence: vocabulary tables, scenario checks, sorting tasks, route maps, flowcharts, screenshots, charts, and short demonstrations.

The main feasibility risks are not conceptual alignment; they are classroom execution risks. Robotics hardware, account access, MakeCode/Tinkercad/Scratch setup, and rubric/template readiness will determine whether the plan feels smooth or overloaded. The third trimester is the tightest because it bridges mBot data, spreadsheets, 3D modelling, and micro:bit in a short formal grading window.

Overall rating: ready with controlled scope and materials prepared.

## Continuity Check

The year sequence is coherent:

1. Internet addresses, packets, collaboration, responsible communication.
2. Website structure, image copyright, links/navigation, Scratch variables.
3. Responsible technology and e-waste.
4. mBot safety, structure, movement, debugging, outputs, loops, sensors, conditions, challenge.
5. mBot project data becomes spreadsheet data.
6. Spreadsheet charts lead into 3D modelling.
7. micro:bit input/output and programming are introduced after students already know variables, outputs, conditions, and sensors.

Continuity strengths:

- Vocabulary appears before or near the task where students use it.
- Most assessments occur after at least one guided practice.
- Robotics lessons build from safety and movement before sensors and conditions.
- Third trimester reuses mBot project data, which helps spreadsheets feel less disconnected.

Continuity risks:

- The annual plan still reads broader than the monthly implementation in places, especially for third-trimester micro:bit and step-counter ambitions. The monthly plan wisely narrows the scope; the annual plan should state that these are small controlled products.
- Older audit files still mention risks or placements that are no longer fully current. Treat the official monthly DOCX files and final assessment map as the current planning truth.

## Dosification And Pacing

Pacing strengths:

- Pre-activities are usually review, sorting, matching, warm-up questions, or short vocabulary work.
- While activities hold the gradeable evidence, which is correct.
- Post activities are usually short closure, reflection, sharing, or submission tasks.
- Most 45-minute classes stay lighter than the 90-minute blocks.
- The plan includes make-up language for hardware/account-dependent work.

Pacing cautions:

- Avoid turning March vocabulary tables into long copying sessions; use simple definitions, examples, and quick illustrations.
- April should not require a polished website and a polished Scratch project. Keep the evidence to the named checks.
- July sensor and condition work needs a reliable sensor choice before teaching.
- August final mBot challenge should grade goal, behavior, test result, improvement, and explanation rather than route perfection.
- October has three counted grades. Keep the chart task, 3D design, and micro:bit input/output check short and rubric-driven.
- December final micro:bit work should remain practice/demo evidence, not a surprise extra formal grade.

## Planning Quality Check

What is working:

- Monthly plans have clear competences, objectives, outcomes, evaluation notes, vocabulary plans, teacher reality notes, and lesson tables.
- Lesson wording is mostly student-facing and action-oriented.
- Evidence is named clearly enough for rubrics: table, quiz, form, route map, flowchart, chart, design plan, sorting check, screenshot, reflection.
- App activities and vocabulary files support the classroom flow instead of adding unrelated work.
- Rubrics exist for all 15 counted summatives.

What still needs tightening:

- Some earlier assessment/reality-check files are out of sync with the current official DOCX placement, especially around third-trimester micro:bit timing. Add a short note that the current official version collects all five formal IIIT grades by October.
- August Summative #5 should be completed in Week 11 and worded consistently everywhere as the teacher-assigned mBot Rescue Robot presentation: line-following code, obstacle and branch logic, rescue-route performance, mBlock use, English explanation, lab care, and readiness/respect evidence. Week 12 remains formative only.
- The annual plan should explicitly include May Internet Day/e-waste as a community technology application, mBot Knowledge Cards Part 1 as a named robotics resource, and controlled small products for spreadsheet/3D/micro:bit work.

- micro:bit device count, USB cables, batteries, or simulator-only plan.
- School calendar interruptions in May, August, October, November, and December.

4. 3D design-plan template and screenshot/export instructions.

## DOCX Format And Compatibility Check

Confirmed across official monthly DOCX plans:

- A4 landscape page setup.
- 0.5 inch margins on top, right, bottom, left, header, and footer.
- No Word numbering/list properties found.
- Green week header shading is present.
- Summative emphasis is present where formal grades appear.

Compatibility issue:

- June, July, and August official DOCX files were flagged by the Word compatibility audit for nameless styles in `word/styles.xml`.
- March, April, May, September, October, November, and December passed the same package audit.

Recommendation: repair June-August styles before final printing/submission, especially if the files will be opened in Microsoft Word for Mac.

## Final Feasibility Decision

The Grade 6 plan is feasible if it is taught as a controlled, evidence-based technology course rather than as a set of large projects. The content density is appropriate for Grade 6 when every formal grade stays small, visible, and supported by prior practice.

The plan should be approved with three guardrails:

1. Keep the five-summative primary structure exactly as written.
2. Do not expand robotics, 3D modelling, or micro:bit into open-ended final projects.
3. Prepare the checklists, make-up paths, account access, and hardware routines before the first class of each tool-heavy unit.

Final status: teachable with manageable risk.
