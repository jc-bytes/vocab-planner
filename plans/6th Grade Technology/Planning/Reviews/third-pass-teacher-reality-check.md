## Classroom Flow Check

Overall, the monthly plans are ready to teach. Each class follows a realistic pattern: short vocabulary or warm-up, one main task, and a closure action. The While Activities are generally the center of the lesson.

Most important flow limits:

- March packet simulation: assign roles before starting and stop with 10 minutes left for closure.
- April website/Scratch work: have accounts and starter files ready; if login fails, use planning sheets.
- May awareness product: limit formats to poster, slide, or infographic.
- June robotics setup: pre-check batteries, number kits, and assign pair roles.
- July Summative #4: give every pair the assigned ultrasonic task, starter project, threshold/response requirements, and equipment checklist before the assessed class.
- August Summative #5: finish and present in Week 11 using the same teacher-prepared Rescue Robot route and provided logic for all pairs; stagger demonstrations by assigned order. Keep Week 12 formative only.
- September formulas: use a prepared spreadsheet; do not make students build the whole file first.
- October 3D modelling: no printing or advanced measurement required.
- November presentations: use one-minute presentations so micro:bit setup is not squeezed.
- December final project: keep the project menu small and collect files/screenshots before presentation day.

## Student Understanding Check

Student understanding is strongest where students first see a model, then complete a guided task, then submit evidence. This pattern is now explicit in the monthly notes for spreadsheet formulas, mBot sensors, 3D modelling, and micro:bit conditions.

## Assessment Balance Check

### First Trimester

Balance is acceptable. There are vocabulary/concept grades, scenario judgment, practical website work, and Scratch programming. The trimester has enough variety, but it depends on May staying as an appreciation/community responsibility month rather than becoming another large project.

Possible risk: March has two concept-heavy grades. This is acceptable because April adds practical website and Scratch evidence.

### Second Trimester

Balance is strong after the mBot rewrite. It includes vocabulary, planning, practical movement, debugging, sensor/condition logic, and a final demonstration. Appreciation evidence appears through collaboration, station notes, and reflection.

Possible risk: hardware availability could make practical assessment uneven. Use paper/emulator alternatives only for make-up, not as the default for all students.

### Third Trimester

Balance is healthy: spreadsheets, charts, 3D planning/building, project presentation/reflection, micro:bit programming, and a final demonstration. There is a good mix of planning/design, practical building, debugging/testing, and explanation.

Possible risk: October and December both include project products. Keep October's 3D work small and December's micro:bit menu controlled.

## Project Readiness Check

### mBot Final Challenge

Ready with conditions. Students practice movement, outputs, loops, sensors, simple conditions, testing, and debugging before the final August challenge.

Required constraints:

- Work in pairs when possible.
- Final challenge must use movement plus one output or one sensor.
- Grade the goal, program behavior, testing, improvement, and explanation, not route perfection.

### 3D Model Product

Ready as a small design/build task, not a full fabrication project.

Required constraints:

- Use at least three shapes.
- Submit a sketch/design plan before building.
- Submit screenshot or saved file.
- No 3D printing required unless the teacher separately confirms time and equipment.

### Final Micro:bit Project

Ready with a small menu of options. Students practice inputs, outputs, variables, conditions, sensors, testing, and explanation before the final project.

Required constraints:

- Project must include one input, one output, and one condition or variable.
- Students choose from alarm, mood display, counter, reaction game, night light, or step-counter-style idea.
- Demonstration is one minute, followed by a short explanation.
