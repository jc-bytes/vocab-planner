# 6° Technology - November 2026

## Monthly Focus

Students finish and share their spreadsheet or 3D work, then begin micro:bit programming with simple inputs, LEDs, and variables.

## Weekly Time

- Class 1: 45 minutes
- Class 2: 90 minutes
- Weekly total: 3 class hours

## Main Resources

- Pen
- pencil
- technology notebook
- student's email and password
- presentation/reflection form
- peer feedback slips
- micro:bit parts worksheet
- input/output sorting check
- micro:bits or MakeCode simulator
- USB cables
- batteries if needed
- projector
- computers/internet

## Competences

- Present a digital or 3D artifact clearly and respond to peer feedback.
- Identify micro:bit parts, inputs, outputs, buttons, display, and basic program behavior.
- Create, test, and explain simple micro:bit programs that use buttons and changing values.

## Learning Objectives

- Prepare a short explanation of one completed project artifact and one improvement.
- Label micro:bit parts and sort examples into input and output.
- Build simple micro:bit programs using display output, button input, and a counter variable.

## Learning Outcomes

- Present one artifact and complete a short project reflection or peer feedback activity.
- Complete a micro:bit parts and input/output check.
- Demonstrate simple micro:bit programs and explain which input or variable was used.

## Evaluation

- Submit a project reflection.
- Complete peer feedback.
- Demonstrate a micro:bit mini-program.
- Complete Summative Activity #4: Label 5 micro:bit parts and sort 8 examples into input or output.

## Teacher Reality Notes

- Main purpose: close the spreadsheet/3D work clearly, then introduce micro:bit inputs, outputs, and variables.
- Grade evidence: Summative Activity #4 is the micro:bit parts/input-output check; grade correct labels and sorting. Project reflection and peer feedback are appreciation evidence; grade completion, respectful feedback, and one specific improvement. The micro:bit mini-program is observed with a checklist: LED output, button or gesture input, test, and explanation.
- Flow guardrail: presentations must stay short so micro:bit setup is not squeezed. Use one-minute presentations and collect reflection immediately after.
- Materials to prepare: presentation/reflection form, peer feedback slips, micro:bit parts worksheet, input/output sorting check, micro:bits or MakeCode simulator, USB cables, batteries if needed, projector, computers/internet.
- Make-up option: reflection and input/output check can be made up on paper; micro:bit demonstration needs a device or simulator slot.
- Risk: transitioning from presentations to micro:bit in the same month can feel abrupt. Use the shared vocabulary of input, output, feedback, and improvement to connect the units.
