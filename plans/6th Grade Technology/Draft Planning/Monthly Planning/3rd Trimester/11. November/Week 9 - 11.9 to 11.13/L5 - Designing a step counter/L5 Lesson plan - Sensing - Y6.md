# Lesson 5: Designing a step counter

Enjoyed teaching these lessons? Found a mistake? Share feedback at the-cc.io/feedback.

## Introduction

Learners will be working at the design level. They will pick out features of a step counter, a piece of technology with which they are likely to be familiar. They will then relate those features to the sensors on a micro:bit. In the main activity, learners will design the algorithm and program flow for their step counter project.

## Learning objectives

To design a project that uses inputs and outputs on a controllable device

- I can decide what variables to include in a project

- I can design the algorithm for my project

- I can design the program flow for my project

## Key vocabulary

Micro:bit, design, task, algorithm, variable, step counter

## Preparation

Subject knowledge:

This lesson draws on the concept of levels of abstraction (LOA) for design in programming. Learners are provided with the task and will be working at the design level. You will need to have a clear understanding of the algorithm part of the design process. An explanation of LOA is included in the unit overview.

An example step counter project is included.

The completed designs from this lesson will be needed for Lesson 6. It would be helpful if feedback could be provided for learners to reflect on at the beginning of Lesson 6.

You will need:

- L5 Slides

- L5 Resource – Step counter design complete

- A1 Activity sheet – Step counter design template (Final designs are required for Lesson 6)

- AP (Optional) – Micro:bits with USB micro leads

## Assessment opportunities

Activity 1: Assess whether learners can identify what variable/s they will need for their program.

Activity 2: Assess whether learners can create their algorithm to match a given task.

Activity 3: Assess whether learners can design their own program flow.

Show learners the task level description of the project they will be undertaking. Discuss any queries they have about the task. |

| Activity 1<br>(Slides 5–7)<br>10 mins | Choosing variables<br>Display slide 5. Show the video, which is an emulation of a micro:bit step counter. The following can be observed in the video:<br>(00:00) Shake movements simulated<br>(00:07) Button B pressed and a value of 27 is displayed<br>(00:10) The message “Keep it up!” is scrolled on the LEDs<br>(00:21) Shake movements simulated<br>(00:26) Button B pressed and a value of 53 is displayed<br>(00:29) The message “Great!” is scrolled on the LEDs<br>Ask learners to note down what they think has happened in this video using the prompts on the slide:<br>Q: What inputs are there?<br>A: There are two inputs — the micro:bit is shaken and the B button is pressed<br>Q: When is a variable changed?<br>A: When the micro:bit is shaken, the variable changes<br>Q: When is a variable displayed?<br>A: When button B is pressed<br>Q: When is a condition used?<br>A: When the messages are displayed, a condition determines which message is displayed<br>Note: You may wish to show the video first, then ask the learners to think about the questions as the video is replayed.<br>Share slide 6. They will need to use these for Activities 2 and 3 of this lesson. The final design should be collected and kept safe for Lesson 6. |
| Activity 2 (Slides 8–10)<br>15 mins | Design your algorithm<br>Display slide 7. Explain that there are questions that the learners need to consider when creating the algorithm for their project. Discuss these questions as a class, ensuring the learners have a good understanding of how their algorithm should look. Remind the learners to think about the steps they have taken in previous lessons, such as initialising their programs.<br>Give the learners time to complete their algorithm for their program.<br>After 5 minutes, display slide 8. They should do this by tracing their flow to each point and determining whether the order is correct.<br>Share slide 10. Explain to the learners that they have used selection in their program to control the program flow. Ask the learners “Where might you see selection used in real-world programs?” Suggestions could include:<br>Elevators<br>Traffic lights |
| Plenary<br>(Slide 11)<br>5 mins | Ways to debug your code<br>Display slide 11. Explain to the learners that they will be creating their programs in the next lesson.
