# Lesson 6: Presenting data

Enjoyed teaching these lessons? Found a mistake? Share feedback at the-cc.io/feedback.

## Introduction

Learners will gain skills to create charts in Google Sheets. They will evaluate the results from their charts to answer questions. Finally, learners will show they understand that there are different software tools available within spreadsheet applications to present data.

## Learning objectives

To choose suitable ways to present data

- I can produce a chart

- I can use a chart to show the answer to questions

- I can suggest when to use a table or chart

## Key vocabulary

Chart, evaluate, results, comparison, questions, software, tools, data

## Preparation

Subject knowledge:

It would be beneficial to have an understanding of how to create charts within spreadsheet applications. An ability to evaluate charts to answer questions would also be an advantage. An understanding of the tools available within spreadsheet applications such as formulas and charts would also be helpful, however, each of these skills is supported in the slides.

You will need:

- Slides

- Dry wipe boards and pens

- A1 resource – Party spreadsheet pie chart example

- A1 resource – Completed party spreadsheet example

- Learners will need access to their own saved spreadsheet from Lesson 5

## Assessment opportunities

Activity 1: Assess the learners’ ability to create a chart.

Activity 2: Assess the learners’ ability to use a chart to show the answer to questions.

Activity 3: Assess the learners’ understanding of when to use a table or chart.

## Outline plan

Please note that the slide deck labels the activities in the top right-hand corner to help you navigate the lesson.

*Timings are rough guides

| Introduction<br>(Slides 2–4)<br>5 mins | Introduction<br>Share the learning objectives on slide 2.<br>Show slide 3. Tell the learners that sometimes when they present data, it is easier for people to understand when it is shown on a chart. Explain that a chart saves people from looking through a detailed spreadsheet and making assumptions.<br>Show slide 4 and explain that in Google Sheets, it is possible to make a range of charts: pie charts, bar charts, column charts, and line charts. |
| --- | --- |
| Activity 1<br>(Slides 5–7)<br>20 mins | Creating charts<br>Show slide 5. Tell the learners that during this lesson they are going to make a pie chart based on the spreadsheet they created in Lesson 5. Open the ‘Party spreadsheet pie chart’ example. Highlight the cell G5 (venue total costs) and drag down to highlight the cells to H10 (budget left over). Click Insert and select Chart. This will automatically insert a pie chart using the data you selected.<br>Show slide 6 and ask the learners, “Can you make a pie chart using the data from the last lesson?”. Allow them time to open their saved spreadsheet from the previous week to create their own pie chart.<br>Once learners have created their pie charts, share slide 7 and ask them to answer the question with a partner. Allow learners some time to have a free discussion with their partner to draw their own conclusions from their pie charts. Some extra questions have been included to support this discussion if necessary. |
| Activity 2<br>(Slides 8–14)<br>10 mins | Tables or charts?<br>Show slide 8. Explain to the learners that they have now used tables (spreadsheet view) and charts to show data. Thinking about their completed party spreadsheet, ask them to think, pair, share: “When might you use a table on a spreadsheet and when might you use a chart?”.<br>Encourage the learners to think about what is the easiest way to display or share this data. Some answers may include:<br>You would use a chart to show the information visually<br>You might use a chart to show the overall costs or figures<br>You might use a table to show a breakdown of all of the costs (for example, the individual cost for each drink)<br>Share slide 9 and explain that tables are helpful when you want to:<br>Sort data (learners will have had some experience of this in Year 4)<br>Use the data to perform different calculations (using formulas)<br>Make changes to the data (adding or taking away items, etc.)<br>View the individual figures<br>Share slide 10 and explain that charts are helpful when you want to:<br>See the data visually<br>See the differences in the data (for example, the difference in spending if someone chose water instead of a can of Coca-Cola)<br>Compare the data<br>Show slide 11. Explain that each way of presenting the data has its advantages and disadvantages, so they need to make sure they are presenting it in the clearest way possible. Tell the learners that there will be three scenarios on the next slides. They need to think, pair, share, whether they would use a spreadsheet table or a chart to show the data. Explain that there is no wrong answer, but they have to justify why they would use this method of presenting.<br>Show the scenario on slide 12:<br>Q: You want to show the teachers how many laptops are available to be booked out.<br>A: A pie chart would be useful to give a quick overview of the proportion of laptops available.<br>Show the scenario on slide 13:<br>Q: You want to show the scores and statistics for the school’s netball team’s season.<br>A: Sports statistics usually contain more information than can be effectively presented in a chart. In addition, sports information is often ranked in order. This can be shown more effectively in a table.<br>Show the scenario on slide 14:<br>Q: You want to show the dietary information for the attendees of a school event.<br>A: This depends on whether you want an overall view (for example, there are 4 vegetarians in a class of 30) or a more detailed view of each attendee’s requirements to see who has a particular food intolerance. For the overall view, a chart would provide a good visual summary. But for more detailed information that relates to each individual attendee, a table would be better. |
| Activity 3<br>(Slides 15–20)<br>5 mins | Spreadsheets quiz<br>Show the question on slide 15. Ask learners to consider the question and write their response on a dry wipe board. Build the slide to show the correct answer, which will also show the formula required. Move on to slide 16 for a further recap of formulas.<br>Show the question on slide 17. Ask learners to consider the question and write their response on a dry wipe board. Build the slide to show the correct answer. Move on to slide 18 to show two examples of functions.<br>Show the question on slide 19. Ask learners to consider the question and write their response on a dry wipe board. Build the slide to show the correct answer. Move on to slide 20 for a recap of why charts are beneficial. |
| Plenary<br>(Slide 21)<br>5 mins | Why do you use spreadsheets?<br>Show slide 21 and encourage learners to think, write, pair, share: “Why do you use a spreadsheet?”. Take some suggestions from the learners and conclude the lesson with a set of ideas. |
