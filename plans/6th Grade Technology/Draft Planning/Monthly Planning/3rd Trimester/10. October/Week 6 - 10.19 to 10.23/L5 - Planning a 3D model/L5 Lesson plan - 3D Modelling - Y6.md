# Lesson 5: Planning a 3D model

Enjoyed teaching these lessons? Found a mistake? Share feedback at the-cc.io/feedback.

## Introduction

Learners will see how computer-based 3D design is used in architecture to plan buildings. They will explode 3D models of buildings to see what shapes they comprise of. Learners will then look at real-world structures and identify the shapes that they include. They will then plan their own 3D building design.

## Learning objectives

To plan my own 3D model

- I can analyse a 3D model

- I can choose objects to use in a 3D model

- I can combine objects in a design

## Key vocabulary

3D shapes, choose, combine

## Preparation

Subject knowledge:

You will need to be familiar with grouping and ungrouping objects in Tinkercad. Tinkercad’s Start Learning 3D tutorials provide a good starting point: https://www.tinkercad.com/learn/designs.

You will need:

- Slides

- A1 – access to:

- the-cc.io/Y6511

- the-cc.io/Y6512

- the-cc.io/Y6513

- A2 Handout – Shapes in architecture

- Desktop or laptop computers with internet connection to access Tinkercad. If using laptop computers, an external mouse is preferable, but not essential.

## Assessment opportunities

Activity 1: Assess learners’ ability to identify 3D shapes in a 3D model produced on a computer.

Activity 2: Assess learners’ ability to to identify 3D shapes in real-world buildings.

Activity 3: Assess learners’ ability to plan their own building designs featuring shapes they have identified in real-world buildings.

## Outline plan

Please note that the slide deck labels the activities in the top right-hand corner to help you navigate the lesson.

*Timings are rough guides

| Introduction<br>(Slides 1–3)<br>5 mins | Planning a 3D model<br>Introduce the lesson and the learning objectives.<br>Show slide 3, explain that 3D design is used by architects to design buildings.<br>Ask learners why they think computer based 3D design is useful in architecture. Learners should be able to draw on their experiences of 3D design in the unit so far to identify some of the following:<br>Designs can be viewed from different perspectives<br>Objects can be easily duplicated, moved, and resized<br>Explain that in this lesson, learners will be planning their own building design which they will create in Tinkercad. |
| --- | --- |
| Activity 1<br>(Slides 4–6)<br>10 mins | Explode a 3D model<br>Explain that the image on slide 4 is of three houses which have been created in Tinkercad. Ask learners what they notice about the 3 houses — they should identify that they get progressively more complex as you view them from left to right. Then ask learners which shapes they can identify in the images. Once suggestions have been made, tell learners that they will be exploding these 3D models to see what shapes and objects they are made up of.<br>Show slide 5. Play the animation to show the house being pulled apart. Tell learners that they will be given links (which are on slide 6) to the three projects so that they can pull them apart themselves. Highlight that some of the projects may have the shapes locked together. To release them, learners need to select the shapes and then click on the padlock symbol, making sure it is left in the open position. |
| Activity 2 (Slides 7–11)<br>10 mins | Choosing shapes<br>Click through the images of architecture with distinctive shapes on slides 7-10. Pause on each image and ask learners what shapes they can see in the building. Learners should identify the following:<br>Slide 7 – The Pyramids: There are a number of square based pyramids. Some learners may apply prior knowledge to identify that each pyramid is made of individual cuboids.<br>Slide 8 – The Taj Mahal: There are a number of shapes including sheres, cylinders, and cuboids. Some learners may also recognise that some of the shapes have been hollowed out.<br>Slide 9 – The Burj Khalifa: Although it appears to be a complex shape, this building is actually made up of many connected cylinders.<br>Slide 10 – A contemporary house: These houses are a series of intersecting cuboids set at different angles.<br>Show slide 11 and give learners the opportunity to browse the shapes available in Tinkercad, to give them ideas for their own building designs. |
| Activity 3<br>(Slide 12)<br>15 mins | Making a plan<br>Ask learners to sketch out a design for a 3D building, explaining that they will be making the buildings in Tinkercad, so it would be advisable for them to use shapes which are available in the application. Emphasise that they should identify and label the shapes they will use in their design, as shown in the example on the slide.<br>Ask learners to think carefully about the proportion of the objects in their design. For example, how big will the windows be compared to the door, or the overall size of the building? |
| Plenary<br>(Slide 13)<br>5 mins | Duplicating buildings<br>Show the image of a row of buildings produced in Tinkercad. Ask learners how they think this has been done. After a brief discussion, explain that an individual building can be grouped, duplicated, and then ungrouped so that individual parts of the building can be duplicated. |
