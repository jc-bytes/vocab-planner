# Lesson 4: Making a desk tidy

Enjoyed teaching these lessons? Found a mistake? Share feedback at the-cc.io/feedback.

## Introduction

Learners will be introduced to dimensions in Tinkercad which will enable them to accurately resize and move shapes. Learners will then be introduced to placeholders which can be used to create holes in objects. Finally learners will duplicate, then resize multiple objects to create a meaningful 3D object.

## Learning objectives

To create a 3D model for a given purpose

- I can accurately size 3D objects

- I can show that placeholders can create holes in 3D objects

- I can combine a number of 3D objects

## Key vocabulary

Cylinder, placeholder, hollow

## Preparation

Subject knowledge:

You will need to be familiar with using 3D objects as placeholders in Tinkercad. Tinkercad’s Start Learning 3D tutorials provide a good starting point: https://www.tinkercad.com/learn/designs.

You will need:

- Slides

- A1 Handout – Accurate measurements

- A2 Handout – Making holes in shapes

- Desktop or laptop computers with internet connection to access Tinkercad. If using laptop computers, an external mouse is preferable, but not essential.

- Homework – What other 3D models could you make?

## Assessment opportunities

Activity 1: Assess learners’ ability to accurately resize objects using measurements in Tinkercad.

Activity 2: Assess learners’ ability to accurately size placeholders to make holes in 3D shapes.

Activity 3: Assess learners’ ability to purposefully duplicate, resize, and combine 3D objects to make a meaningful object.

## Outline plan

Please note that the slide deck labels the activities in the top right-hand corner to help you navigate the lesson.

*Timings are rough guides

| Introduction<br>(Slides 1–3)<br>5 mins | Making a desk tidy<br>Introduce the lesson and the learning objectives.<br>Show the image of a 3D printed desk tidy on slide 3. Tell learners that they will be making their own version of a desk tidy, digitally.<br>Ask learners what 3D modelling techniques they think have been used to make this model. Learners should identify that cylinders have been placed, duplicated, moved, and resized. |
| --- | --- |
| Activity 1<br>(Slides 4–9)<br>10 mins | Accurate measurements in Tinkercad<br>Show slide 4. Explain to learners that in order to make a model like a desk tidy, they will have to use accurate measurements. Highlight that the image on the slide is a close up of the workplane, and that each small square on the workplane is 1 mm x 1 mm or 1mm², and each large square is 10mm x 10mm or 1cm².<br>Show slide 5. Explain that if you move the mouse over one of the resizing handles, the dimensions of an object are shown. Pose the questions on the slide. Learners should identify that 42.00 means 42mm, that you can estimate an object's size by using the grid lines on the workplane, and that you view the height by moving the mouse to the handle which adjusts the height of an object. This is demonstrated on slide 6.<br>Show slide 7. Explain that in addition to using the handles to resize an object, you can also click on the field where the dimension is shown and enter a value. This can make it easier to make an object a particular size.<br>Show slide 8. Remind learners that when lifting objects, the distance of the object from the workplane is shown in mm. If the object is placed below the workplane, this will be a negative value.<br>Show slide 9 and ask learners to complete the shapes in Tinkercad. They will have enough space on the workplane to make all three objects. |
| Activity 2 (Slides 10–14)<br>10 mins | Making holes in shapes<br>Show the image on slide 10. Explain that the desk tidy is a 3D printed object which consists of a number of connected cylinders. Unlike the shapes learners have worked with so far, these cylinders have been hollowed out to create containers. They still have a base which forms the bottom of each container.<br>Show slide 11. Explain that shapes can be hollowed out using 3D placeholders. Talk the learners through the process of making holes in shapes. You may also wish to demonstrate the process in Tinkercad. The process is as follows:<br>Add a solid cylinder to your workplane. Note the dimensions (width, depth and height).<br>Add a placeholder cylinder to your workplane. Reduce the dimensions to slightly smaller than your solid cylinder.<br>Lift the placeholder off the workplane by about 2mm to create the base of your shape.<br>Move the placeholder onto the cylinder and position it evenly on all sides.<br>Select and group the two objects. You should now see a hollowed out cylinder.<br>Ask learners to follow the steps in the slide (and on the handout) to create their own hollowed out cylinder. |
| Activity 3<br>(Slides 15–16)<br>15 mins | Duplicating and resizing<br>Show slide 15. Remind learners of the desk tidy image they saw at the beginning of the lesson. Highlight that the desk tidy is made of six connected, hollowed out cylinders. Explain to learners that once they have successfully made one cylinder, they can repeat the process, but change the dimensions to make the remaining cylinders, or they can duplicate it and resize their first cylinder.<br>Show the image of a 3D model of a desk tidy on slide 16. Give learners time to create their own version in Tinkercad. |
| Plenary<br>(Slide 17)<br>5 mins | What other 3D models could you make?<br>Ask learners to consider what other objects could be represented as 3D models. If learners need guidance, highlight features of the desk tidy, in particular hollowed out shapes. In general, look for responses which are static objects with no moving parts. |
| Next time<br>(Slides 18–19)<br>5 mins | This lesson, next lesson<br>Review the ‘Assessment’ and ‘Summary’ slides. |
| Homework<br>(optional) | What other 3D models could you make?<br>Building on the activity in the plenary, ask learners to consider other objects which they could 3D model. Encourage learners to use their surroundings either in school or at home for ideas. |
