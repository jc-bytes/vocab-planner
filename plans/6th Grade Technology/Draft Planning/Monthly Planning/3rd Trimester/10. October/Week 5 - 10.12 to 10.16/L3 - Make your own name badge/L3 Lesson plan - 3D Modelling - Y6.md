# Lesson 3: Make your own name badge

## Introduction

Learners will develop their understanding of manipulating digital 3D objects. They will rotate objects in three dimensions, duplicate objects, and then use grouping and ungrouping to manipulate many objects at once. They will combine these skills to create their own 3D name badge. Finally, learners will consider the practicality of 3D printing the objects they have made.

## Learning objectives

To recognise that objects can be combined in a 3D model

- I can rotate objects in three dimensions

- I can duplicate 3D objects

- I can group 3D objects

## Key vocabulary

Rotate, duplicate, group

## Preparation

Subject knowledge:

You will need to be familiar with rotating, duplicating, and grouping 3D objects in Tinkercad. Tinkercad’s Start Learning 3D tutorials provide a good starting point: https://www.tinkercad.com/learn/designs.

You will need:

- Slides

- Desktop or laptop computers with internet connection to access Tinkercad. If using laptop computers, an external mouse is preferable, but not essential.

## Assessment opportunities

Activity 1: Assess learners’ ability to use the rotate handles in three dimensions, and their ability to lift and lower shapes back onto the workplane.

Activity 2: Assess learners’ ability to duplicate objects and manipulate them in a variety of ways.

Activity 3: Assess learners’ ability to use grouping to combine objects and modify them with a single action.

## Outline plan

Please note that the slide deck labels the activities in the top right-hand corner to help you navigate the lesson.

*Timings are rough guides

| Starter activity<br>(Slides 1–3)<br>5 mins | Introduction<br>Introduce the lesson and the learning objectives.<br>Explain that in this lesson, learners will experiment with more types of shapes and more ways of moving them. Tell learners that in addition to shapes, they can also add text and draw objects freehand. Ask learners if they can identify which shape on slide 3 is hand-drawn. After a brief discussion, reveal that the ‘@’ sign is hand-drawn. |
| --- | --- |
| Activity 1<br>(Slides 4–6)<br>10 mins | Rotating shapes<br>Tell learners that in addition to moving and resizing objects, you can also rotate them. Explain that to rotate a shape, you need to select it and use the curved double-headed arrows which are circled on the image on the screen.<br>Note: Depending on the angle the shape is viewed from, the rotate handles are not always easy to see. If you cannot see one or more of the handles, try changing the viewing angle.<br>Ask learners to add some text by clicking on ‘TEXT’ in the basic shapes section. Once they have added some text, they can edit the placeholder to use their own text.<br>Show slide 5. Ask learners to use the rotating handles to rotate their text in three dimensions.<br>Once learners have experimented with rotating their text, show slide 6. Most learners will have rotated their objects in such a way that some of it appears below the workplane. Explain that they can use the lift/lower handle to place the object back on to the workplane. Highlight that when using the lift/lower tool, the distance between the object and the workplane is shown, with a negative number meaning the base of the object is below the workplane. |
| Activity 2 (Slides 7–8)<br>10 mins | Duplicating objects<br>Explain to learners that you can use an object more than once by duplicating it. Duplicating can be done by selecting an object and clicking on the duplicate button (which is shown on the slide) or by pressing Ctrl +D on the keyboard.<br>Move on to slide 8. Challenge learners to create an object and duplicate it. Once they have have duplicated it, ask them to use the techniques they have been introduced to in this and the previous lesson to edit their shapes by:<br>Resizing<br>Lifting/lowering<br>Rotating<br>Changing colour |
| Activity 3<br>(Slides 9–11)<br>15 mins | Make your own name badge<br>Show the image on slide 9. Explain that the model includes a cuboid for the base, hand-drawn letters, and star shapes which have been duplicated. Tell learners that they will be making their own version of a name badge — they can use this example for ideas, but they should also use their own ideas.<br>Move on to slide 10. Explain that if you want to change the size or move something which is made up of a number of objects, then it needs to be grouped.<br>Show slide 11 and explain the steps needed to group an object. Highlight that once an object is grouped, it will show as all the same colour. If it is then subsequently ungrouped the original colours will be shown. To group objects, learners need to:<br>Select the items you want to group by clicking and dragging a box around them.<br>Click on the ‘Group’ button<br>Once items are grouped, learners will be able to move and resize them as one object.<br>Give learners time to create their name badges. Allow time for saving and/or screenshots if you want to record evidence of learners’ work. |
| Plenary<br>(Slides 12–13)<br>5 mins | Could it be printed?<br>Explain to learners that for an object to be printable, each layer needs to sit on the layer below. This means that objects cannot have overhangs as the printer would have to print parts of the model in mid-air.<br>Ask learners if they think the model shown on the screen could be printed. After a discussion, explain that it could not, because there is no layer for the text and stars to print on top of. Ask learners to consider if their models could be printed.<br>Move on to slide 13. Explain that in this case, rotating the shape by 90 degrees will make it printable, as once it has been rotated there are no overhangs. |
