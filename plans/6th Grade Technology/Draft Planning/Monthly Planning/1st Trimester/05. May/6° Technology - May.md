# 6° Technology - May 2026

## Monthly Focus

Students connect technology with community responsibility through Internet Day and recycling. Students discuss, research, and create a simple awareness product.

## Weekly Time

- Class 1: 45 minutes
- Class 2: 90 minutes
- Weekly total: 3 class hours

## Main Resources

- Pen
- pencil
- technology notebook
- student's email and password
- e-waste sorting cards or Google Form
- awareness product checklist
- safe research links
- poster/slide/infographic template
- projector
- internet access

## Competences

- Make responsible technology choices for safety, kindness, privacy, and reliable information.
- Explain simple ways to reduce technology waste through reuse, repair, recycling, and e-waste sorting.
- Create and share a short awareness product for a clear audience.

## Learning Objectives

- Sort online actions and technology items into safe or responsible categories using practiced vocabulary.
- Identify practical habits that protect people, devices, and the environment.
- Plan, create, and present a simple poster or slide with a clear message and supporting facts.

## Learning Outcomes

- Complete vocabulary and sorting activities about responsible internet use and technology waste.
- Complete a recycling and e-waste sorting check with explanations for selected items.
- Submit and present a short awareness product with a topic, audience, message, and three key words or facts.

## Evaluation

- Complete vocabulary activities.
- Complete recycling and e-waste sorting tasks.
- Submit an awareness product.
- Complete Summative Activity #5: Sort 10 technology items into reuse, repair, recycle, or e-waste, then explain the best responsible choice for 2 of the items.

## Teacher Reality Notes

- Main purpose: connect responsible technology use with Internet Day and recycling/e-waste action.
- Grade evidence: Summative Activity #5 is the completed sorting check; grade correct category choices and the two responsible-choice explanations. The awareness product is appreciation evidence, not a major technical grade; observe participation, clarity, and respectful presentation.
- Flow guardrail: keep research to three facts or tips only. Do not let the awareness product become a large design project.
- Materials to prepare: e-waste sorting cards or Google Form, awareness product checklist, safe research links, poster/slide/infographic template, projector, internet access.
- Make-up option: the sorting check and three-fact list can be completed independently; the one-minute presentation can be made up to the teacher or a small group.
- Risk: Week 3 can run long if students choose too many formats. Offer only poster, slide, or infographic unless there is extra time.
