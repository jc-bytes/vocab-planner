# Lesson 3: Working together

Enjoyed teaching these lessons? Found a mistake? Share feedback at the-cc.io/feedback.

## Introduction

Learners consider how people can work together when they are not in the same location. They discuss ways of working and complete a collaborative online project. The online activity assumes that learners can make simple slides, including text and images. If your learners are unsure how to do this, you may wish to spend some time on the Year 3 – ‘Desktop publishing’ unit before this lesson.

Note: This lesson uses Google Slides, a free web-based app, which is part of Google Workspace for Education. You will need a school Google account. If your school doesn’t have Google Workspace, you can sign up for a free account. Alternative collaborative presentation tools are available, such as Microsoft PowerPoint used with Office 365, if you wish to use different software.

## Learning objectives

To explain how sharing information online can help people to work together

- I can recognise how to access shared files stored online

- I can send information over the internet in different ways

- I can explain that the internet allows different media to be shared

## Key vocabulary

Chat, explore, slide deck

## Preparation

Subject knowledge:

- Learners will need to access a template in a shared online space. You will need to modify or create templates for each of the groups. An example is provided and will need to be duplicated. Each copy will need to be named with a different country, and then shared with your learner groups.

- If you have never set up Google Workspace for Education, there is some guidance on how to do it here.

- You will need to know how to create a presentation file that can be shared among a group of learners working on different computers at the same time; there is some support for Google Slides here.

- You will need to make each group a copy of the presentation that has a different country’s name on it. Only share the presentation with the learners who will use it.

- You will need to know how to add simple text and images to slides.

You will need:

- L3 Slides

- A2 Teacher resource – Country fact file (one per group)

- A2 – Computers that are connected to the internet, ideally one per learner

- A2 – Individual learner logins for Google Workspace or other collaborative software

- L3 Homework – Working together (optional)

## Assessment opportunities

Activity 1: Assess whether learners can show an understanding of the benefits and limitations of different technological solutions to a problem.

Activity 2: Assess whether learners can demonstrate their ability to find online content (text and images) to use on their slides, and show how to arrange them effectively.

Activity 3: Assess whether learners can reflect on the challenges of collaborating online.

## Outline plan

Please note that the slide deck labels the activities in the top right-hand corner to help you navigate the lesson.

*Timings are rough guides

| Introduction<br>(Slides 3)<br>5 mins | Review<br>Show slide 3. Ask learners to think about the previous lessons and talk to a partner about these questions:<br>What factors are important if you want to communicate successfully?<br>Why should you use addresses?<br>What are small parcels of digital information called?<br>After a few minutes, ask the class for their answers. Encourage learners to explain their answers, which may include:<br>Agreed ways of communicating (agreed protocols)<br>So the message gets to the right recipient and can be replied to, and/or the message source can be checked<br>Packets<br>Build the slide to explain that in this lesson, learners will look at how communication over the internet can benefit us. |
| --- | --- |
| Activity 1<br>(Slides 4–5)<br>5 mins | Working together over a distance<br>Share slide 4. Explain to the learners that people who are not in the same location may need to work together.<br>Give the learners time to consider how people can work together over long distances. If necessary, prompt them to consider solutions:<br>Contact each other using computers<br>Phone each other<br>Write to each other<br>Travel to meet in person<br>Show slide 5. Ask the learners to consider the pros and cons of each option. They should discuss issues such as cost, time, and ease of working. |
| Activity 2 (Slides 6–8)<br>25 mins | Working on a collaborative project online<br>Note: The example activity provided is to produce a fact file about a chosen country. You may wish to ask your learners to focus on something more suited to their current topics for this activity. If you decide to adapt the resources, you will need to amend slide 6–8 accordingly.<br>Show slide 6. Explain to the learners that they will work together in small groups to complete this activity. However, they will not be working at the same table as their group members. Tell the learners they need to produce a fact file about a country. The learners need to remain in their seats and only use the online tools provided to collaborate with their group. Explain that this is because they are working as if they are in different locations.<br>Share slide 7. Show learners where the Chat option is. Learners may also need to know about using the Explore button to find information and images. Images can be inserted directly from the Explore panel.<br>Display slide 8. Explain that the information pack will take the form of a shared slide deck. Learners need to access their group’s shared slide deck and use the Chat tool to communicate with each other. The starter project slide deck has an index page and several other pages for different pieces of information about the country.<br>Allow time for learners to find and use the Google Slides tools and then to work together on their project. |
| Activity 3<br>(Slide 9)<br>5 mins | Review working online<br>Show slide 9. Ask the learners to review the activity and compare it to the ways of working discussed at the start of the lesson. Ask them to think, pair, share to discuss the questions on the slide.<br>Ask, “What media types have you had to share to get your slides looking as they do now?”. Potential answer: learners shared text in the chat, along with images and facts that they found online.<br>Ask, “What other media types could you include in your slides?”. Potential answers: links, video, graphics, and sounds.<br>Ask, “Could you share those things in the ways mentioned earlier in the lesson (e.g. on the phone or by travelling)?”. Potential answers:<br>Phone call: you can chat, but you can’t share files.<br>Travelling: you can talk and share text and images, but it would have taken much longer. |
| Plenary<br>(Slide 10)<br>5 mins | Reflection<br>Show slide 10. Ask the learners to reflect on their shared working task so far. Ask them to think about the questions on the slide:<br>How do you feel after collaborating online today?<br>What went well?<br>What could you improve next time?<br>Note down some of the responses and keep them for the beginning of the next lesson. |
