# Lesson 6: Communicating responsibly

Enjoyed teaching these lessons? Found a mistake? Share feedback at the-cc.io/feedback.

## Introduction

Learners use information provided in the lesson and their own prior knowledge to categorise different forms of internet communication. They then choose which method(s) they would use for the scenarios discussed in the previous lesson. Through these activities, learners explore issues around privacy and information security.

## Learning objectives

To evaluate different methods of online communication

- I can compare different methods of communicating on the internet

- I can decide when I should and should not share information online

- I can explain that communication on the internet may not be private

## Key vocabulary

Communication, public, private, one-way, two-way, one-to-one, one-to-many

## Preparation

Subject knowledge:

You will need to be familiar with a range of internet-enabled methods of communication, especially the eight methods discussed in this lesson. The notes in the solutions to Activity 1 are a useful prompt for discussions.

You will need:

- L6 Slides

- A0 – Individual whiteboards

- A1 Activity sheet – Comparing methods of internet communication (slides 2–3 can be cut and distributed amongst groups if preferable)

- A1 Solutions – Comparing methods of internet communication

- A1 – Sticky notes

- AP Activity sheet – Exit ticket (pre-cut, one for each learner)

- Learners’ mind maps from Lesson 5

## Assessment opportunities

Introduction: You can assess learners’ familiarity with a variety of different forms of communication.

Activity 1: You can assess how well learners can categorise different forms of communication according to the criteria given, and through their application of prior knowledge.

Activity 2: You can assess how well learners can apply their knowledge of methods of communication to choose the most appropriate method(s) and justify their choices.

Plenary: You can assess how well learners can explain what they should consider before they choose how to communicate.

Ask learners to consider the following questions and write their answers on their whiteboards:<br>When have you communicated with someone in the last week?<br>Where were they, compared to you?<br>How did you communicate with them?<br>Share slide 4. Ask learners to stand up if the statement is true for them:<br>Have you video called someone?<br>Have you communicated with someone in another country?<br>Have you messaged someone on a gaming platform?<br>Have you shared a picture with someone else? |

| Activity 1<br>(Slides 5–13)<br>25 mins | Comparing methods of internet communication<br>Show slide 5. Build the slide and ask learners to think, pair, share some methods of internet communication, recapping the previous lesson. After learners have responded, build the slide to reveal eight methods of internet communication and explain that today’s lesson will focus on these methods.<br>Display slide 6. Explain to the learners that they will have time to complete the table shown, to compare methods of communication and they will be given some cue cards to support them. Build the slide and ask them to think about what each column means.<br>Share slide 7. Outline each of the categories shown in the table header by moving through the slides (slides 7 to 12):<br>Public or private? (slide 7): Public communication is visible to all; private communication may be restricted to certain individuals or groups. Many forms of internet communication (such as Twitter) have settings to define how public or private their service is for individual users.<br>What can you share? (slide 8): This refers to different types of media: text, images, video, and audio.<br>One-to-one or one-to-many? (slide 9): Is the communication with one person or many people? Some services, such as WhatsApp, enable both.<br>One-way or two-way? (slide 10): Is communication one-way or two-way? Most internet communication is two-way, however there are examples of one-way communication, such as YouTube videos or blog posts that do not allow comments.<br>Are there adverts? (slide 11): Is the service funded by advertising? This builds on what the learners found out in the Year 5 – ‘Computing systems and networks’ unit about how search engines make money.<br>Age limit? (slide 12): Does the service have an age limit? If so, what is it?<br>Display slide 13. Provide the learners with the activity sheet and share out the accompanying cue cards (these can be shared one each, or cut up and shared amongst a group to share). If required, complete some selected table cells as a class to demonstrate the process to learners.<br>You can find the completed table in the solutions sheet. This sheet includes notes that provide further clarification for a number of the responses. These notes can act as useful discussion prompts. |
| Activity 2 (Slide 14)<br>15 mins | Choosing a method of communication<br>For this activity, ask learners to return to the groups that they were in for Activity 3 in Lesson 5. Share the mind maps from Lesson 5 with each group.<br>Tell the learners to use sticky notes, or edit their work, to include how their scenario fits some of the criteria from Activity 1 of this lesson:<br>Public or private?<br>Should it be shared?<br>One-to-one or one-to-many?<br>One-way or two-way?<br>Then, ask groups to decide which method(s) of communication they think their scenario is best suited to.<br>Give each group the opportunity to share their decision with the rest of the class. Stress that the learners should explain why they have made their choices. |
| Plenary<br>(Slide 15)<br>5 mins | Exit ticket<br>Display slide 15 and give each learner an exit ticket. Ask them to reflect on what they have learnt in this lesson. They should decide on the three most important things that they should consider when choosing how to communicate with someone using the internet. |
