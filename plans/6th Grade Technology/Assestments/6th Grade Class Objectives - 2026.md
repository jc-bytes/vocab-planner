# 6th Grade Technology Class Objectives - 2026

These objectives are based on the 6th Grade Technology annual plan and the March-December draft monthly plans. The same objectives can be used for 6A, 6B, and 6C when the sections follow the same monthly schedule.

## Trimester 1

### March

| Week | Class | Topic | Class Objective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | 45 minutes | Internet addresses | Students will be able to identify a domain name and explain, with a simple example, how DNS helps a browser find a website. |
| Week 1 | 90 minutes | Domain names and IP addresses | Students will be able to connect domain names with IP addresses and complete a vocabulary table for core internet communication terms. |
| Week 2 | 45 minutes | Data packets | Students will be able to describe the header and payload of a data packet and explain why long messages are sent in smaller parts. |
| Week 2 | 90 minutes | Packet simulation | Students will be able to model packet delivery, rebuild a message in order, and explain one problem caused by missing or out-of-order packets. |
| Week 3 | 45 minutes | Working together online | Students will be able to sort online tools by purpose and explain one benefit and one challenge of online collaboration. |
| Week 3 | 90 minutes | Shared digital work | Students will be able to contribute to a shared digital file and use respectful online comments or messages to support group work. |
| Week 4 | 45 minutes | Responsible communication | Students will be able to distinguish public and private communication and identify audience and permission needs in simple online scenarios. |
| Week 4 | 90 minutes | Website features and planning | Students will be able to answer responsible communication scenarios, identify website features, and sketch a web page plan for a clear audience and purpose. |

### April

| Week | Class | Topic | Class Objective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | 45 minutes | Copyright and images | Students will be able to distinguish safe and unsafe image use and explain why source and credit matter. |
| Week 1 | 90 minutes | Finding useful images | Students will be able to select a copyright-friendly image, record its source, make a safe-use decision, and write a credit line. |
| Week 2 | 45 minutes | Web page structure | Students will be able to identify basic web page parts and organize a title, section, text, and image for a specific audience and purpose. |
| Week 2 | 90 minutes | Build and preview | Students will be able to build a simple Google Sites page, preview it in different views, and improve two details for readability. |
| Week 3 | 45 minutes | Links and navigation | Students will be able to explain why navigation matters, map how pages connect, and plan one useful link for a web page. |
| Week 3 | 90 minutes | Add links and evaluate | Students will be able to add and test a web page link, evaluate the page with a checklist, and improve it after peer feedback. |
| Week 4 | 45 minutes | Scratch variables | Students will be able to define a variable and predict how a score variable changes in a Scratch game example. |
| Week 4 | 90 minutes | Scratch score challenge | Students will be able to create a Scratch score task with one variable and two clickable sprites, test and debug it, and explain how the score changes. |

### May

| Week | Class | Topic | Class Objective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | 45 minutes | Internet Day: responsible choices | Students will be able to sort online actions into safe, unsafe, or unsure categories and write one responsible internet rule for sixth graders. |
| Week 1 | 90 minutes | Internet Day campaign | Students will be able to create a short Internet Day poster or slide with one rule, one example, and a supporting image or icon. |
| Week 2 | 45 minutes | Recycling and e-waste | Students will be able to sort technology items into reuse, repair, recycle, or e-waste and explain why some items should not go in regular trash. |
| Week 2 | 90 minutes | Technology and the environment | Students will be able to complete an e-waste sorting check and collect three facts or tips about reducing technology waste. |
| Week 3 | 45 minutes | Plan awareness product | Students will be able to plan an awareness product with a clear audience, message, title, key words, and action step. |
| Week 3 | 90 minutes | Create and present | Students will be able to create and present a short awareness product that includes a title, three facts or tips, one image or icon, and one action step. |

## Trimester 2

### June

| Week | Class | Topic | Class Objective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | 45 minutes | Robotics routines and safety | Students will be able to identify mBot safety routines and explain how the rules protect people, devices, and classroom materials. |
| Week 1 | 90 minutes | Meet the mBot | Students will be able to label the main mBot parts, explain each part's function, and complete a robotics vocabulary table. |
| Week 2 | 45 minutes | Instructions and movement | Students will be able to order movement commands and predict the route created by a simple mBot command sequence. |
| Week 2 | 90 minutes | First mBot movements | Students will be able to run a basic mBot movement program, change one value such as time or speed, and record what changed. |
| Week 3 | 45 minutes | Debugging movement | Students will be able to identify likely fixes for simple mBot movement problems and write one useful debugging tip. |
| Week 3 | 90 minutes | Path challenge 1 | Students will be able to correct simple mBot movement sequences, program a short path, test it in sections, and improve one movement. |
| Week 4 | 45 minutes | Robot outputs | Students will be able to choose an LED or buzzer signal for a robot state and explain how outputs help robots communicate. |
| Week 4 | 90 minutes | Movement plus signals | Students will be able to add an LED or buzzer signal to a movement program, test whether it happens at the correct time, and improve its clarity. |

### July

| Week | Class | Topic | Class Objective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 5 | 45 minutes | Movement review | Students will be able to draw a short mBot route map with ordered movement steps and labeled turns. |
| Week 5 | 90 minutes | Path challenge 2 | Students will be able to create a route map and command plan with at least four movement steps, program the route, and debug one accuracy issue. |
| Week 6 | 45 minutes | Loops as repeated actions | Students will be able to identify repeated robot actions and rewrite a repeated command list using loop thinking. |
| Week 6 | 90 minutes | Repeat patterns | Students will be able to program a repeated movement pattern, test and adjust one value, and explain how a loop makes the program shorter. |
| Week 7 | 45 minutes | Sensors around us | Students will be able to identify a robot sensor, describe what it detects, and connect it to a real-life sensor example. |
| Week 7 | 90 minutes | mBot sensor practice | Students will be able to test a sensor in two situations, record what changed, and create one simple sensor response. |
| Week 8 | 45 minutes | Simple conditions | Students will be able to create an if/then flowchart with one condition, one true response, and one false response. |
| Week 8 | 90 minutes | Ultrasonic condition practice | Students will be able to create a practice ultrasonic flowchart, match it to if/else code, test one response, and correct one problem. |
| Week 9 | 45 minutes | Prepare for Summative 4 | Students will be able to interpret the teacher-assigned ultrasonic task and organize the required flowchart, code, explanation, and equipment evidence. |
| Week 9 | 90 minutes | Summative 4 - Ultrasonic Sensor Task | Students will be able to complete the assigned ultrasonic flowchart and code, demonstrate two responses, submit the project, and explain the logic in English. |

### August

| Week | Class | Topic | Class Objective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 10 | 45 minutes | Line-following logic review | Students will be able to identify the loop, line-sensor input, corrections, and lost-line response in the assigned Rescue Robot code. |
| Week 10 | 90 minutes | Guided line-following practice | Students will be able to test and correct the line-following section of the assigned Rescue Robot project. |
| Week 11 | 45 minutes | Rescue Robot final build and rehearsal | Students will be able to finish the assigned Rescue Robot logic, test the full route, correct one problem, and prepare the explanation and equipment checklist. |
| Week 11 | 90 minutes | Summative 5 - Rescue Robot presentations | Students will be able to present and demonstrate the assigned Rescue Robot, submit the project, explain the behavior in English, and return the kit correctly. |
| Week 12 | 45 minutes | Formative Rescue Robot reflection and feedback | Students will be able to reflect on the presentation and identify one successful decision, one challenge, one debugging step, and one possible improvement. |
| Week 12 | 90 minutes | Formative robotics review and equipment readiness | Students will be able to review line-following, sensors, conditions, flowcharts, and debugging while preparing the numbered kits for the next unit. |

## Trimester 3

### September

| Week | Class | Topic | Class Objective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | 45 minutes | Inspect ready mBots | Students will be able to inspect mBot readiness evidence and identify one useful data field for a second STEM challenge. |
| Week 1 | 90 minutes | IIIT vocabulary and challenge plan | Students will be able to complete an IIIT vocabulary table and plan a second mBot challenge spreadsheet with useful data fields. |
| Week 2 | 45 minutes | Collect mBot data | Students will be able to collect fair mBot challenge data and record clean records with complete information. |
| Week 2 | 90 minutes | mBot STEM Data Table | Students will be able to create a clean mBot STEM data table with at least six records, headers, units or categories, readable formatting, and one explanation sentence. |
| Week 3 | 45 minutes | Formulas with mBot data | Students will be able to use a spreadsheet formula to calculate mBot data and explain the input and output of the calculation. |
| Week 3 | 90 minutes | Formula practice with project data | Students will be able to calculate totals or averages from project data, change one input value, and describe how the output changes. |

### October

| Week | Class | Topic | Class Objective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | 45 minutes | mBot charts tell a story | Students will be able to identify chart titles, labels, and categories and choose one mBot data question that can be answered with a chart. |
| Week 1 | 90 minutes | mBot Data Chart and Conclusion | Students will be able to create one chart from mBot STEM data, answer three chart questions, and write one conclusion based on evidence. |
| Week 2 | 45 minutes | 3D modelling basics | Students will be able to create and manipulate a basic 3D shape and explain what it means to work in three dimensions. |
| Week 2 | 90 minutes | Shape practice | Students will be able to build a simple object from basic shapes using move, resize, rotate, and duplicate tools. |
| Week 3 | 45 minutes | Plan a simple model | Students will be able to sketch a simple 3D model, label at least three shapes, state its purpose, and identify one possible improvement. |
| Week 3 | 90 minutes | 3D Model Design | Students will be able to create a 3D model design with a sketch, at least three labeled shapes, a purpose, one planned improvement, and screenshot or model evidence. |
| Week 4 | 45 minutes | Meet the micro:bit | Students will be able to label basic micro:bit parts and sort examples into inputs and outputs. |
| Week 4 | 90 minutes | micro:bit Parts and Input/Output Check | Students will be able to complete a micro:bit parts and input/output check and create a simple display or button program. |

### November

| Week | Class | Topic | Class Objective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | 45 minutes | Prepare to share | Students will be able to prepare three talking points about a spreadsheet, chart, 3D model, or project artifact and improve one point after feedback. |
| Week 1 | 90 minutes | Present and reflect | Students will be able to present project work in one minute, listen respectfully to classmates, give one positive comment, and complete a reflection. |
| Week 2 | 45 minutes | Meet the micro:bit | Students will be able to identify basic micro:bit parts and explain one input and one output example. |
| Week 2 | 90 minutes | First micro:bit programs | Students will be able to create a micro:bit program that shows text or an icon and uses button A or B as an input. |
| Week 3 | 45 minutes | Variables on micro:bit | Students will be able to plan a micro:bit counter program by choosing a variable, input button, and LED display behavior. |
| Week 3 | 90 minutes | Counter program | Students will be able to create a micro:bit counter variable, increase and display its value, and explain how the variable changed. |

### December

| Week | Class | Topic | Class Objective |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | 45 minutes | Sensors and conditions | Students will be able to create an if/then flowchart for a sensor condition and explain the output that should happen. |
| Week 1 | 90 minutes | Sensor response program | Students will be able to create a sensor response flowchart and guided micro:bit program using light or temperature input with different outputs. |
| Week 2 | 45 minutes | Plan final interactive project | Students will be able to plan a simple interactive micro:bit project with an input, output, condition or variable, and success rule. |
| Week 2 | 90 minutes | Build and test final project | Students will be able to build and test a final micro:bit project using familiar blocks, record one problem, and improve one behavior. |
| Week 3 | 45 minutes | Prepare explanation | Students will be able to prepare a one-minute explanation that correctly uses input, output, condition, variable, and improvement vocabulary. |
| Week 3 | 90 minutes | Demonstrate and close | Students will be able to demonstrate the final micro:bit project, explain its input, output, and condition or variable, and complete a final reflection and concept check. |
