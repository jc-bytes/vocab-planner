# Exam: STEM Prototype — Build, Test, Improve, and Demonstrate

Trimester 3 · 90 points  
Formal demonstrations: 9A Tuesday, November 17 · 9B Wednesday, November 18  
These demonstrations occur during the November 17–21 Grade 9 review/project week. November 24–27 is reserved for academic exams and closure; no required Technology evidence follows November 21.

## Purpose

Build a STEM prototype, model, or digital product that addresses the approved problem. Test it against measurable criteria, improve it using evidence, and demonstrate the final result.

Your approved ungraded proposal milestone and assigned card from `../Assessment Sources and Templates/05 STEM Proposal Option Cards.md` are the starting plan. The card supplies the problem, users, product choices, required test inputs, and knowledge source. Do not search for a new topic. The project may change after testing, but the teacher must approve a major change and every change belongs in the logbook.

## Required evidence

1. Assigned option card, approved proposal, problem, users, goal, tool, materials, labeled design, and criteria `C1–C3`
2. Checkable prototype, model, or digital product
3. Saved working file or teacher-approved share link with clear version names
4. Project logbook with dated work, decisions, feedback, and next actions
5. At least three controlled test trials using the three required option-card inputs
6. One identified weakness supported by test evidence
7. One improvement connected to that weakness and a comparison result
8. Final demonstration notes and individual reflection

## Safety and evidence rules

- Use only the option card, named module sections, and teacher-approved tools, materials, accounts, and data. No open-web research is required.
- Do not collect unnecessary personal data.
- Use saved computer work, typed records, and live teacher observation as evidence.
- Save the actual working file, a teacher-approved share link, or a typed test record.
- Ask before changing the approved problem, platform, or major success criteria.

## Final demonstration

In 1–2 minutes, show the product and explain the problem, users, tools, one design choice, three test results, one evidence-based improvement, one limitation, and what you learned.
