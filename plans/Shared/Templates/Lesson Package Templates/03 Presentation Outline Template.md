# Presentation Outline

## Direct-Instruction Deck

1. Class title, objective, and expected evidence.
2. Retrieval question or warm-up.
3. Why the topic matters or connection to prior learning.
4. Key concept with visual explanation.
5. Modeled example.
6. Guided check for understanding.
7. Student task and exact requirements.
8. Success checklist and submission instructions.
9. Work checkpoints, timer, and troubleshooting prompts.
10. Exit question and next step.

## Short Work-Session Deck

1. Objective and today's milestone.
2. Starting point and required files.
3. Success checklist.
4. Work checkpoints, timer, and troubleshooting.
5. Save, submit, and closure instructions.

## Presentation QA

- [ ] The deck title is short, literal, and easy to understand.
- [ ] Student-facing text uses familiar words, short sentences, and direct verbs.
- [ ] Each instruction shows one main action at a time.
- [ ] Necessary technical terms are explained with a simple definition or example.
- [ ] Slide content matches the teacher guide and handout.
- [ ] Required counts, dimensions, filenames, and submission rules match the directions.
- [ ] Text and visuals are readable from the classroom.
- [ ] No private answer key, scoring notes, or student data appears.
- [ ] Shared decks clearly identify the slides for each class.
