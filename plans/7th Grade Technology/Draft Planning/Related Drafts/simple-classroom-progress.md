# 7th Grade Technology - Simple Classroom Progress

This file explains the year in simple classroom sentences. It is meant for quick teacher review, parent-friendly explanation, or student-facing overview.

## 1st Trimester

### March

- Week 1, Class 1: First, students work on mBot components and robot parts. They sort real or pictured mBot parts into structure, movement, sensor, and control categories without adding a separate vocabulary table.
- Week 1, Class 2: Then, students work on mBot assembly, safety, and the Trimester 1 summative vocabulary. They complete Daily Grade #1: Create a vocabulary table with definition, illustration, and example sentence for these 10 words: mBot, hardware, software, chassis, motor, sensor, LED, buzzer, controller, control board.
- Week 2, Class 1: Then, students work on Sensors, motors, and control board. They practice labeling an mBot parts diagram and draft function sentences before the graded check.
- Week 2, Class 2: Then, students work on mBlock workspace and first scripts. They complete Daily Grade #2: Label an mBot parts diagram with at least 8 parts and write one function sentence for 5 of the parts. They also drag movement blocks into the scripts area and test a simple sprite movement sequence.
- Week 3, Class 1: Then, students work on Connecting mBlock commands to robot actions. They connect the mBot to the computer, test basic commands, and practice identifying mBlock workspace areas before the graded interface check.
- Week 3, Class 2: Then, students work on First movement program. They complete Daily Grade #3: Label a screenshot of the mBlock interface with at least 6 areas and write one sentence explaining how blocks become robot instructions. They also program the mBot to move in a small square or triangle path.
- Week 4, Class 1: Then, students work on Robots as systems. They create a simple system map showing how the mBot control board, sensors, motors, and mBlock program work together.
- Week 4, Class 2: Then, students work on Movement sequence practice. They build a movement sequence in mBlock for a taped floor route and complete Appreciation Grade #1 for safe material use and teamwork.

### April

- Week 5, Class 1: Then, students work on Speed, time, and distance. They practice testing speed and time values and prepare the calibration chart for the long class.
- Week 5, Class 2: Then, students work on Precise turns and measured paths. They complete Daily Grade #4: Create a movement calibration chart with 3 speed values, 3 time values, observed distance, and one conclusion sentence about how speed and time affect movement. They also program the mBot to complete a square path.
- Week 6, Class 1: Then, students work on Path planning on a grid. They draw a route map with start point, finish point, 2 checkpoints, and at least 6 movement arrows.
- Week 6, Class 2: Then, students work on Efficient navigation. They complete Appreciation Grade #2: Complete a self-reflection about effort, organization, receiving feedback, and perseverance during testing and debugging.
- Week 7, Class 1: Then, students work on LED states and meaning. They program green for ready, blue for moving, and red for stop.
- Week 7, Class 2: Then, students work on Movement with LEDs and sound. They combine movement, LED, and buzzer blocks in one program.
- Week 8, Class 1: Then, students work on Ultrasonic sensor values and thresholds. They complete a 6-question quiz-style check on sensor values, thresholds, and if/else choices.
- Week 8, Class 2: Then, students work on If/else obstacle detection. They complete Daily Grade #5: Program and demonstrate an mBot obstacle reaction that moves forward when clear, detects a nearby object, stops, shows a red LED or plays a sound, and includes one spoken explanation of the if/else logic.

### May

- Week 9, Class 1: Then, students work on Maze vocabulary and route planning. They sketch a maze route with start, finish, at least 2 turns, and 2 obstacles.
- Week 9, Class 2: Then, students work on Maze prototype and final project preparation. They build a practice maze section with available classroom materials.
- Week 10, Class 1: Then, students work on Begin Exam Project: mBot Maze Navigator. They begin Exam Project: Create the project plan with maze sketch, robot start/finish, checkpoints, required sensors, planned movement sequence, and team responsibilities.
- Week 10, Class 2: Then, students work on Build and program maze sections. They continue Exam Project: Finalize the maze section, program the first navigation algorithm, test the mBot, record trial results, and debug speed, timing, turns, or thresholds.
- Week 11, Class 1: Then, students work on Debugging and improvement plan. They continue Exam Project: Use a debugging checklist to fix one movement issue, one sensor issue, or one sequence issue. Retest and record whether the change improved the result.
- Week 11, Class 2: Then, students work on Final testing and documentation. They continue Exam Project: Run the full maze or best available maze section, document the final program, collect trial evidence, and prepare a 1-2 minute explanation of the solution and challenges.
- Week 12, Class 1: Then, students work on Presentation rehearsal. They rehearse the demonstration and explanation using the rubric so the final presentation is ready for the long class. They check that the robot, program, and maze are ready.
- Week 12, Class 2: Then, students work on Final maze demonstration. They complete Exam Project Presentation: Demonstrate the mBot Maze Navigator, explain how the code controls the robot, describe how sensors support decisions, identify one challenge and solution, and submit the project reflection.

## 2nd Trimester

### June

- Week 1, Class 1: Then, students work on Clear digital messages. They analyze one environmental poster using a checklist with 2 strengths, 2 weaknesses, audience, purpose, main image, title, and one improvement suggestion without adding a separate vocabulary table.
- Week 1, Class 2: Then, students work on poster planning, copyright-free images, and the Trimester 2 summative vocabulary. They complete Daily Grade #1: Create a vocabulary table with definition, illustration, and example sentence for these 10 words: audience, purpose, poster, Canva, contrast, alignment, readability, copyright-free, Creative Commons, credit. They then plan an environmental poster and build a first draft in Canva.
- Week 2, Class 1: Then, students work on Poster revision. They practice revising the environmental poster and check the title, message, image, readability, colors, and credit before the graded submission.
- Week 2, Class 2: Then, students work on Branding and consistent design. They complete Daily Grade #2: Create and submit an environmental poster in Canva with a clear title, one main message, one appropriate image, readable text, intentional colors, and image credit/source evidence.

### July

- Week 3, Class 1: Then, students work on Brand consistency in slides. They practice building a branded slide set and check the design before the graded submission.
- Week 3, Class 2: Then, students work on How computers follow instructions. They complete Daily Grade #3: Create a 3-slide branded mini-presentation with consistent logo placement, colors, readable text, one image or icon, and one sentence explaining how the design supports the message. They then open Scratch and explore sprites, scripts, events, motion blocks, looks blocks, and sound blocks.
- Week 4, Class 1: Then, students work on Scratch sequence and music. They practice building a Scratch sequence and test the order before the graded task.
- Week 4, Class 2: Then, students work on Variables for changing data. They complete Daily Grade #4: Build a Scratch sequence with at least 8 blocks, one event block, one sound or costume change, and one written explanation of why the order matters. They also create a simple counting or score program in Scratch.
- Week 5, Class 1: Then, students work on Selection and if/else logic. They build a Scratch response program that asks a question and uses if/else to give two different responses.
- Week 5, Class 2: Then, students work on Comparison and logic operators. They complete a 6-question quiz-style check on comparison and logic operators.
- Week 6, Class 1: Then, students work on Count-controlled loops. They replace repeated Scratch blocks with a repeat loop.
- Week 6, Class 2: Then, students work on Loops and variables together. They complete Appreciation Grade #1: Complete a peer feedback checklist about respectful comments, specific suggestions, participation, and response to feedback. They also create a countdown or rhythm-timing program that uses a loop and a variable.
- Week 7, Class 1: Then, students work on Debugging strategies. They practice identifying Scratch errors and planning fixes before the graded debugging check.
- Week 7, Class 2: Then, students work on Independent problem solving with Scratch. They complete Daily Grade #5: Debug a Scratch starter program by identifying 3 errors, fixing at least 2 errors, and submitting a short debugging note that names the problem, fix, and result.

### August

- Week 8, Class 1: Then, students work on Scratch concept review. They complete a short practice task that adds one input, one condition, and one variable to a Scratch starter file.
- Week 8, Class 2: Then, students work on Dance game preparation. They complete Appreciation Grade #2: Complete a pair-programming self-reflection about driver/navigator roles, effort, communication, organization, and responding to feedback. They also build a practice dance move with one key press, one costume change, one sound, one movement, and one success/failure response.
- Week 9, Class 1: Then, students work on Project rubric and planning. They create a project plan with game goal, keys used, dance moves, variables, subroutines, success/failure feedback, and pair-programming responsibilities.
- Week 9, Class 2: Then, students work on Prototype and testing checklist. They create a prototype checklist for the dance game.
- Week 10, Class 1: Then, students work on Begin Exam Project: Scratch Dance Game. They begin Exam Project: Set up the Scratch project, name variables, identify sprites/costumes, and create the first dance move subroutine.
- Week 10, Class 2: Then, students work on Build dance moves and input logic. They continue Exam Project: Build at least two dance move routines, add success/failure feedback, test user input timing, and debug one code issue.
- Week 11, Class 1: Then, students work on Score, timer, and feedback. They continue Exam Project: Improve the score, timer, and feedback subroutine. Test whether the game responds correctly to correct and incorrect actions.
- Week 11, Class 2: Then, students work on Final game improvement and presentation prep. They continue Exam Project: Complete the required features, debug the game, prepare a 1.5-2 minute demonstration, and write a short real-world application explanation.
- Week 12, Class 1: Then, students work on Presentation rehearsal. They rehearse the game demonstration and code explanation using the rubric so the final presentation is ready for the long class.
- Week 12, Class 2: Then, students work on Final dance game demonstration. They complete Exam Project Presentation: Demonstrate the Scratch Dance Game, explain subroutines, input, conditionals, loops, variables, and one real-world application, then submit the reflection.

## 3rd Trimester

### September

- Week 1, Class 1: Then, students work on spreadsheet introduction. They label a simple spreadsheet screenshot and practice finding cells without adding a separate vocabulary table.
- Week 1, Class 2: Then, students work on entering data, formatting, and the Trimester 3 summative vocabulary. They complete Daily Grade #1: Create a vocabulary table with definition, illustration, and example sentence for these 10 words: spreadsheet, sheet, row, column, cell, cell reference, formula, function, average, chart.
- Week 2, Class 1: Then, students work on Basic formulas. They practice formulas using a small data set and discuss responsible data teamwork before the graded reflection.
- Week 2, Class 2: Then, students work on Charts and data interpretation. They complete Daily Grade #2: Create a spreadsheet with at least 8 data rows, use 3 formulas, create 1 chart, and write 2 interpretation sentences explaining what the chart shows.

### October

- Week 3, Class 1: Then, students work on Sorting and filtering data. They sort and filter a small spreadsheet data set.
- Week 3, Class 2: Then, students work on Conditional formatting and COUNTIF. They complete Appreciation Grade #1 for data teamwork and apply conditional formatting to highlight values that meet a rule.
- Week 4, Class 1: Then, students work on Decomposition and subroutines. They create a Scratch plan that separates a larger action into at least 3 labeled subroutines.
- Week 4, Class 2: Then, students work on Scratch loops and subroutines. They complete Daily Grade #3: Build and demonstrate a Scratch program that uses at least 1 subroutine or custom block, 1 loop, 1 event, and 1 explanation sentence about how decomposition improves the program.
- Week 5, Class 1: Then, students work on Lists and organized data in Scratch. They create a small Scratch list with at least 5 related items.
- Week 5, Class 2: Then, students work on Credible sources and plagiarism. They complete Daily Grade #4: Evaluate 3 online sources using author/date/evidence/purpose, choose 1 credible source, record image or text credit information, and write 2 sentences explaining why the source is usable.
- Week 6, Class 1: Then, students work on Blog and media features. They plan a short cause-based blog section with title, audience, main idea, one image idea, and one source credit.
- Week 6, Class 2: Then, students work on Building a short media post. They create a short Google Sites blog-style post with heading, short paragraph, image, source credit, and call to action.
- Week 7, Class 1: Then, students work on Micro:bit sensor systems. They draw a simple system diagram for a micro:bit project that uses a sensor input to trigger an output.
- Week 7, Class 2: Then, students work on Mandrake Detection System preview. They build a project concept map showing parts, sensor reading, trigger condition, output sound, and one safety or reliability concern.

### November

- Week 8, Class 1: Then, students work on Sensor-system planning. They practice planning a micro:bit sensor system before the graded design plan.
- Week 8, Class 2: Then, students work on Threshold testing practice. They complete Daily Grade #5: Create a micro:bit sensor-system design plan with project purpose, input, process, output, threshold rule, parts list, simple diagram, and 2 testing questions. They also practice reading distance values with classroom equipment when available.
- Week 9, Class 1: Then, students work on Project rubric and readiness. They discuss project-readiness examples before the graded reflection.
- Week 9, Class 2: Then, students work on Make-up, review, and project setup. They complete Appreciation Grade #2: Complete a project-readiness self-reflection about preparation, organization, effort, safe equipment use, receiving feedback, and improving work. They also complete a 10-question quiz-style review on spreadsheet, Scratch, source credibility, and micro:bit vocabulary.
- Week 10, Class 1: Then, students work on Begin Exam Project: Mandrake Detection System. They begin Exam Project: Create the final project plan with purpose, parts, input/output diagram, threshold rule, code plan, test plan, and group responsibilities for 2-3 students.
- Week 10, Class 2: Then, students work on Build and test the sensor logic. They continue Exam Project: Build the micro:bit sensor logic with classroom equipment when available, test distance readings, adjust the threshold, and document results in a test table.
- Week 11, Class 1: Then, students work on Debugging and reliability. They continue Exam Project: Debug one reliability issue and retest the system. Record what changed, what improved, and what still needs work.
- Week 11, Class 2: Then, students work on Final build and presentation preparation. They continue Exam Project: Finalize the system, collect final test evidence, create a Canva or Google Slides support slide, and practice a 1-1:30 minute explanation. Use a clearly labeled simulation only if equipment is unavailable.
- Week 12, Class 1: Then, students work on Presentation rehearsal. They rehearse the demonstration and explanation using the rubric so the final presentation is ready for the long class.
- Week 12, Class 2: Then, students work on Final Mandrake Detection System presentation. They complete Exam Project Presentation: Present the Mandrake Detection System, explain purpose, parts, sensor reading, threshold rule, Bluetooth signal, output sound, challenge, result, and realistic improvement.

### December

- Week 13, Class 1: Then, students work on Exam/admin buffer. They complete a make-up submission, final reflection, or file organization task only if class meets and the teacher assigns it.
- Week 13, Class 2: Then, students work on Exam/admin buffer. They complete a quiet review, reflection, make-up presentation, or administrative task only if class meets and the teacher assigns it.
