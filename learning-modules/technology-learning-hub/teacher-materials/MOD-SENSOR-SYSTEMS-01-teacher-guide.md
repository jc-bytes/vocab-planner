# Sensor Systems, Thresholds, and Testing teacher guide

**Module:** MOD-SENSOR-SYSTEMS-01 v0.1.0  
**Grades:** Grades 7-8  
**Status:** Pilot  
**Evidence:** Saved attempts, application record, and exported HTML practice report

## Before class

- Open the assigned module route and verify the required section IDs.
- Prepare micro:bit and sensor hardware or MakeCode simulator.
- Decide how many practice activities students must complete. Each practice page contains five and the page records retries.
- Keep the printable fallback ready.
- Remind students that progress stays on the current browser and device. They should export before changing computers.

## Teaching pattern

1. Use 5 minutes for the objective and one retrieval question.
2. Teach one Learn section for 10-15 minutes while students follow on their computers.
3. Model the first decision without giving the remaining practice answers.
4. Assign the matching Practice section for 20 minutes.
5. Review one common error. Students then complete or update the application record.
6. Use the final 5 minutes to export evidence or record the stopping point.

Do not assign the whole module in one period. Monthly plans name the exact sections for each class.

## What to check

Check the input-process-signal-output chain, threshold boundary, and test table.

## Misconceptions

Students confuse signal with output; skip the exact boundary value; change several variables at once.

## Differentiation

- Ask students who need support to explain one decision aloud before selecting an answer.
- Let students retry after reading the feedback. A failed attempt remains in the history.
- Students ready for extension should justify why one rejected option fails or create a new test case using the same rule.

## Make-up and fallback

An absent student completes the same assigned Learn and Practice sections, then exports the report. If the site, account, software, internet, or hardware is unavailable, Use the printed diagram and threshold tables. A labeled paper or MakeCode simulation preserves the logic objective.

## Pilot notes

Record actual completion time, unclear wording, repeated wrong answers, device problems, and whether the fallback produced comparable evidence. Keep the module at pilot status until classroom observations have been reviewed.
